# Manuscript Revision Philosophy

The reasoning discipline behind Erfan's line-by-line manuscript feedback (distilled 2026-09-01 from the abstract rewrite, 474 -> 350 words). These are the durable rules; the individual edits are not worth remembering.

**1. Pin the message before touching the words.** His highest-value intervention was "what is the thing or message we want to say here? first let's clarify that." Every time wording was optimized before the claim was fixed, the work had to be redone. Order: reader's question -> the claim the recorded evidence supports -> then phrasing.

**2. One job per sentence, and it must earn its place.** He reliably catches sentences that duplicate a neighbour's claim, that follow arithmetically from what precedes them (the reader already drew the conclusion), or that float as a free-standing maxim. When two sentences overlap, one goes or their roles split.

**3. Connection is content, not style.** His recurring question is "how does this connect to the previous sentence?" A `so` or `therefore` resting on no established relationship is a *logical* defect, not a stylistic one. Two facts placed side by side are not a reason. A term cannot appear without introduction, and a qualifier cannot point at a comparison the text never set up.

**4. Structure beats inventory, the deepest note.** "doesn't feel like an abstract, it feels like we are reporting a list of the things we have done." N verdicts at one rhetorical level, ordered by the sequence the experiments ran, is a chronology. Prose needs a spine: prerequisite -> pivot -> consequence -> verdict -> contribution, with one sentence a reader could quote as *the* finding. Corollary: check what the thesis already says about itself (the Discussion's own opening punchline) before inventing a framing.

**5. Prose devices become tics on repetition.** Label-colon-expansion used twice running reads as headings with inlined bullets ("what is the ...: asdsadsad format"). Root cause was chasing a quotable pivot and turning the pivot into a title. Give the facts, then draw the inference from them.

**6. Honesty is a boundary that shortening may never cross.** `not demonstrated` != `absent`. `does not establish` != `is not`. `did not reliably improve` != `did not matter`. Never state an established null. Re-check every compression against the recorded evidence state (SUPPORTED / NOT DEMONSTRATED / UNRESOLVED / NON-IDENTIFYING); when a shorter phrasing would overclaim, keep the longer one or say nothing.

**7. Verify against records, never memory, including a subagent's.** Trace every asserted claim to `file:line`. In this session a fable reviewer miscounted its own word total and only half-fixed one objection; both were caught by checking. Subagent output is a proposal to argue with, not a result to merge.

**8. Answer the question that was asked, tersely.** "Is this factually correct?" wants a verdict with citations, not a rewrite. "Shouldn't we drop it?" wants yes or no first.

**9. A prose defect may have an upstream cause; look before rewriting.** Section 1 asserted a universal stage order that the repo contract and Section 2 both refuse. The paragraph was not careless: leaf 5 of the question tree literally asked for "ordered evidence-chain stages", so fixing only the prose would have been undone by the next audit, which reads the tree as the target. When prose contradicts a contract, check whether its owning question, terminology table, or leaf told it to.

**Why:** the corrections he had to repeat were almost never about vocabulary; they were about editing at the wrong layer, polishing words while the claim, the connective, or the overall shape was the actual defect.

**How to apply:** in any `/write` or read-through pass, before proposing a sentence, state what claim it must carry and how it attaches to its neighbours. Also: he asks for no lint/gate/render runs between edits during a read-through, only when a re-render is wanted.
