# Push Harder and Watch Availability Bias

On 2026-06-18, during the precedent-engine design session, Erfan said I had been "extremely inefficient and lazy" and "not pushing enough / not rigorous enough." The substantive catch: my counter-critique panel used "we only searched for precedents 2-3 times in the thesis" as evidence the need was small. That is availability or latent-demand bias. You cannot observe demand for an instrument that did not exist; high cost makes people substitute, which hides the demand.

**Why:** using our own possibly suboptimal past process as the gold-standard denominator for "what a researcher needs" measures the wrong thing. Absence of a past request is not absence of need when the tool to satisfy it was missing.

**How to apply:** when judging whether a capability is needed, ask "was this absent because unneeded, or because the instrument was too expensive to use?" before concluding the need is small. Default to more rigor and decisiveness, less hedging; do the substantive work, such as running searches, reading sources, and doing the analysis, rather than producing armchair takes. Challenge hard, but do not capitulate either: concede the valid logical point and hold what genuinely survives.
