# Manuscript Readability Lessons

Erfan's sentence-level feedback on the v0.2 manuscript section 1 rewrite, given on 2026-06-29. The overall approach was good: one-job sentences, explicit Problem/Hypothesis/Contributions signposts, and a contributions list. These are the fixes.

1. **Avoid staccato over-fragmentation.** "One idea per sentence" must not become three choppy sentences for one thought. Merge closely related items into one clean sentence; split only for genuinely distinct claims.
2. **Do not make abstract nouns the acting subject.** "Two axes organize the field" reads as a mouthful and echoes the agency problem. Prefer a plain, often question-shaped framing, such as "The work splits along two questions: ...".
3. **Use plain concrete words over literary or vague ones.** "Loose appeal to brain data" made him stop to decode. If a phrase needs decoding, replace it with something like "a vague argument for using brain data".
4. **Do not coin compound jargon.** "Confound-clean protocol" was an invented label. Describe the thing plainly, such as "a protocol that controls for the artifacts that can fake the score".
5. **Avoid the word "confound" in prose.** It is field-standard, but not to his taste in writing. Use "artifact" or "spurious cue" where possible, including in manuscript-wide titles.
6. **Cut restatement and meta-commentary.** Do not end a paragraph by re-saying its opening, and do not add "these results show..." sentences that merely restate findings. Watch for the same content stated twice across nearby sections.
7. **Subsection titles should be short plain topic labels.** Avoid long, comma-spliced, editorializing titles. Put the editorial point in the body, not the heading.
8. **Avoid conversational or reader-directed meta.** Phrases like "so its scope needs a careful reading", "it is worth noting", "as we will see", and "recall that" are Claude-to-Erfan register, not academic prose. State the substance instead.
9. **Avoid the "This is X, not Y" contrastive frame.** Erfan calls it "absolutely claude language". State the fact plainly, for example "That study measures alignment under compression but never optimizes it." A short appositive contrast can be fine; the tell is a whole sentence built around the X-not-Y frame.

**Why:** readability is judged by a human read, not by passing the writing doctrine or linter. Doctrine-clean prose can still be hard to process; the remedy is often merge and simplify, not more splitting.

**How to apply:** carry these into every Round-3 section and the section 2/section 3 flow pass. These build on the writing doctrine in the adjacent memories.
