# R07: Plain perplexity-only knowledge distillation does not preserve a teacher's brain alignment by default. A from-scratch logit-KD student lands far below its teacher, but how much of that drop is specific to the compression objective rather than to the loss of language-model quality is not resolved at this benchmark

**Answers Q1. Verdict: ✅ PARTIAL.** Knowledge distillation trains a small student to imitate a larger teacher by matching the teacher's output probabilities, rather than learning from raw text, and the question here is whether a student trained that way keeps the teacher's brain alignment. It does not, not for free. The governing variable is how much of the student's representation was *inherited* from ordinary pretraining versus *rebuilt* from the teacher's outputs alone: the more a model had to rebuild, the less alignment it kept. Along that ordering alignment falls monotonically, from a conventional same-size GPT-2 that matches the teacher, through warm-started and real published distillations, down to a from-scratch student trained on the teacher's logits alone, which sits a significant $\Delta=+0.018$ below the teacher ($p<0.001$; it retains only 0.37 of the teacher's alignment above an untrained floor, where 1.0 would be full retention) [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The full five-point ladder is in the evidence table below. So the outcome that would have made the thesis's distillation question a non-problem, *that compression preserves alignment by default*, is ruled out: perplexity-only KD leaves real alignment headroom below the teacher. What the gradient does *not* establish is the stronger claim that the distillation objective sheds alignment beyond the language-model quality it costs. Across the trained and distilled models alignment is tightly predicted by log-perplexity (Pearson $r=-0.88$), so quality alone could account for the whole gradient; the two contrasts that would separate an objective-specific loss from "alignment just tracks quality" are only marginal ($p\approx0.09$), and the from-scratch student is itself under-trained, so part of its low alignment is simply that it is a worse language model [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md) [L011](../learnings.md).

This is a finding-report under the report convention (D036): one claim, tagged with the question it answers, stating current truth only. It sits one rung above R06, which established that the alignment signal is *real* beyond confounds; R07 asks the first question that follows once it is real, whether an aggressive but capability-preserving transform of the model *keeps* it. The answer reframes the thesis's distillation question from a preserve-versus-destroy binary into two quantities: how much alignment a compressed student forfeits, and how much of that forfeit is the compression objective rather than lost capability. R07 reuses R06's measurement apparatus unchanged (unique R² on the Tuckute ROI screen, contiguous splits, a capacity-fair nuisance floor); it adds only the experimental contrast and the statistic that sizes the gap. The discovery order, including a preliminary "F1 confirmed" read that an adversarial refutation later deflated to this, lives in [`map.md`](../map.md), `timeline/`, and `decisions/`, not here. Sources: `experiments/E003`; lesson L011; the apparatus of R06 (`experiments/E002`).

## The question

Does standard perplexity-only knowledge distillation into a smaller student *preserve* or *shed* the teacher's brain alignment, and if it sheds, is the loss specific to the distillation objective or merely a side effect of the student being a worse language model?

The question matters because it locates the thesis. The program lives in the shrink-and-optimize cell: can an alignment-guided objective recover, in a compressed student, brain alignment that the perplexity objective alone does not? [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). If plain KD already preserves alignment for free, that cell is empty: there is nothing to recover and the distillation framing collapses. If plain KD sheds alignment, the size of the shed is the *headroom* an alignment-guided objective could in principle recover, and the cell is open and motivated. Oota et al. measured preservation for quantization and pruning; it was unmeasured for KD, which is the more destructive transform: quantization and pruning perturb a fixed function, whereas KD refits a smaller function from scratch against the teacher's outputs [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

## The design (and the statistic that sizes the gap)

The quantity R07 is after is the **headroom** $\Delta = A_T - A_S$: the distance between the teacher's alignment $A_T$ and the perplexity-only-KD student's alignment $A_S$, both measured as unique R² on the apparatus R06 defines. The estimand is that gap; the estimator is a paired bootstrap over folds and seeds, decided on $\Delta$ with a confidence interval and on a well-conditioned retention statistic, never on a raw point ratio; the identifying assumption is that the verdict-bearing arm carries only alignment that was *transmitted through the distillation channel*, which is what the initialization-trap fix below buys [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

Why a gap and not a binary. For a *deterministic post-processing* of a fixed teacher (the quantization and pruning Oota et al. studied), the student representation $Z_{\text{student}}$ depends on the stimulus only through the teacher features $Y_{\text{teacher}}$, the Markov chain $B \to S \to Y_{\text{teacher}} \to Z_{\text{student}}$ holds, and the data-processing inequality ([`06-theory-grounding.md`](../06-theory-grounding.md) §2, Information Theory for ML lecture 6) bounds what the student can carry about brain activity $B$,

$$
I(Z_{\text{student}};\,B) \;\le\; I(Y_{\text{teacher}};\,B),
$$

so in that regime $A_S \le A_T$: a fixed-function compression can only lose alignment, never create it, and a student that loses *some* is near-certain. That is the ceiling the thesis sets KD against, and it is why the scientific content is *how much* is lost, not *whether*. The empirical finding that alignment survives such compression (a compression counter-paper) says only that for a fixed-function transform the loss is small in practice [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md); it does not settle KD.

Knowledge distillation is not that regime, and this is what makes the gap an empirical question rather than a corollary. The verdict arm is a cold-init student fit by gradient descent on the wikitext corpus against the teacher's logits, so it has a side-channel to the stimulus that bypasses $Y_{\text{teacher}}$ (the raw next-token targets and BPE inputs), the Markov premise fails, and DPI does not bound $A_S$ by $A_T$ for it: a from-scratch student could in principle pick up stimulus structure the teacher's features do not encode. The same gap appears from the objective's side: KD minimizes KL on the *output* distribution (perplexity-only training and KD are the same KL objective against different references — the empirical data versus the teacher's softened logits; [`06-theory-grounding.md`](../06-theory-grounding.md) §6), and the many-to-one representation→logits readout leaves that output match free to be realized by a less brain-aligned internal geometry, so preservation is not forced a priori. DPI says the ceiling stops bounding the student; the objective says nothing pulls the student back to it either — which is exactly why the preservation question is empirical, not a corollary. That the alignment ceiling is not a hard bound on the measured numbers is visible directly in the table below, where conventional gpt2 ($A_S=+0.0195$) scores numerically *at or above* the larger gpt2-medium ($A_T=+0.0188$) [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). So R07 does not lean on DPI to predict the gap; it measures the gap directly, with DPI standing only as the fixed-function ceiling the KD result is read against. Informally the picture is rate–distortion (`06` §4): the student sits at a rate (its parameter budget) and pays an alignment-distortion $\Delta=A_T-A_S$ relative to the teacher, and the question Q2/Q3 take up is how favourably that distortion trades against rate; R07 measures one operating point per arm, not the curve, and keeps that rate–distortion picture distinct from the DPI ceiling above (they are different statements, L013).

**The initialization trap, and the fix that makes the verdict arm trustworthy.** The cheap obvious design, fine-tuning a *pretrained* small student toward the teacher with KD, is a trap: a pretrained GPT-2 student already sits at full teacher-grade alignment (R06's E002 measures it there), so a high retention would only say "a few KD epochs did not erase the alignment the student was born with from pretraining." That is fine-tuning drift, not distillation, and the DPI argument is about a function fit from scratch against teacher outputs, which a warm start never instantiates [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The fix, locked before running: the verdict-bearing arm is a **cold-init** (randomly initialised) student trained only on the teacher's logits, the one arm where any alignment it ends with was transmitted through the KD channel rather than inherited. Warm-init KD is kept as a labelled fine-tuning-drift control, and a no-teacher plain-LM-finetune arm separates corpus drift from the KD objective itself [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

The arms, all scored on the R06 apparatus (Tuckute 2024, five left-hemisphere language ROIs, NC-normalised unique R², contiguous five-fold CV, capacity-fair PCA), with the verdict comparison held entirely within 12-layer GPT-2-architecture models at a fixed layer (L7) so there is no cross-architecture layer confound and no winner's-curse layer selection [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md):


| Tag         | Model                    | Role                                     | Init / objective           |
| ----------- | ------------------------ | ---------------------------------------- | -------------------------- |
| teacher     | gpt2-medium (24L, 355M)  | the DPI ceiling $A_T$                    | pretrained                 |
| gpt2        | gpt2 (12L, 124M)         | conventional same-architecture reference | pretrained                 |
| **kd_cold** | gpt2 ← KD(gpt2-medium)   | **the verdict arm**                      | **random + pure logit KD** |
| kd_warm     | gpt2 ← KD(gpt2-medium)   | fine-tuning-drift control                | pretrained + pure logit KD |
| lmft_warm   | gpt2 ← plain-LM finetune | corpus-drift control (no teacher)        | pretrained + plain LM      |
| distilgpt2  | distilgpt2 (6L, 82M)     | a real distillation, for context         | distilled-from-gpt2        |
| untrained   | gpt2 (random)            | the floor $A_0$                          | random                     |


The statistic decided on is **floor-anchored retention** $\rho' = (A_S - A_0)/(A_T - A_0)$, with $A_0$ the untrained floor and $A_T$ the teacher: $\rho'=0$ means "fell back to the floor" (destroyed), $\rho'=1$ means "kept the teacher's alignment" (preserved). It is far better conditioned than a raw ratio near the cutoffs, and the verdict is read on $\rho'$ with its bootstrap CI together with $\Delta$ and a paired test, never on a point ratio [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The KD objective is the purest perplexity-only form (temperature-scaled KL on next-token logits over the shared BPE vocabulary, $\lambda_{\text{brain}}=0$, $\lambda_{\text{hidden}}=0$), purer than distilgpt2, which adds a hidden-state cosine term that would protect mid-layer structure; distilgpt2 is therefore scored against its own teacher (gpt2, not gpt2-medium) and read as context, not verdict [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). Stop rule, locked: fixed model set, fixed verdict layer, three seeds on the trained arms, a matched corpus, batch size, learning-rate schedule and max-length, and no tuning toward an outcome [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). As-run the optimizer-step count was *not* matched: the from-scratch cold arm trained two epochs to the warm arms' one, so it received about twice the steps, which only deepens the under-training caveat below rather than flattering it [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

## The evidence

The verdict-layer table, NC-normalised unique R² (NC $=0.353$), floor-anchored retention $\rho'$ with bootstrap 95% CI, $\Delta = A_T - A_S$ versus the teacher, the bootstrap probability of no drop from the teacher, and held-out perplexity [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md):


| Arm                 | init / objective                           | unique R² | $\rho'$ [bootstrap CI] | $\Delta$ vs teacher | $p$(no-drop) | ppl           |
| ------------------- | ------------------------------------------ | --------- | ---------------------- | ------------------- | ------------ | ------------- |
| teacher gpt2-medium | pretrained                                 | +0.0188   | n/a                    | n/a                 | n/a          | 74 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)     |
| gpt2                | pretrained (conventional)                  | +0.0195   | 1.02 [0.77, 1.27]      | $\approx 0$         | 0.57         | 105 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)    |
| lmft_warm           | warm + plain LM finetune                   | +0.0174   | 0.95 [0.81, 1.13]      | +0.0016             | 0.265        | 44 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)     |
| kd_warm             | warm + pure logit KD                       | +0.0144   | 0.84 [0.67, 1.04]      | +0.0046             | 0.061        | 95 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)     |
| distilgpt2          | real distillation (6L/82M, +hidden-cosine) | +0.0076   | 0.60 [0.42, 0.84]      | +0.0119             | 0.001        | 169 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)    |
| **kd_cold**         | **random + pure logit KD**                 | +0.0005   | 0.37 [0.14, 0.58]      | +0.0181             | 0.000        | 477 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)    |
| untrained           | random (floor)                             | −0.0102   | 0                      | n/a                 | n/a          | ~50000 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md) |


The durable finding is a **monotone alignment gradient**. Ordered by how much the representation was rebuilt through the distillation channel rather than inherited from conventional pretraining, retention falls cleanly: conventional gpt2 ($\rho'\approx1.0$) &gt; warm-init KD (0.84) &gt; full distillation distilgpt2 (0.60, an own-teacher anchor) &gt; from-scratch cold-init KD (0.37) &gt; floor (0). The from-scratch logit-KD student lands far below the teacher ($\Delta=+0.018$, $p<0.001$); it sits significantly above the untrained floor ($\rho'=0.37$, CI excluding 0) yet its absolute unique R² is statistically indistinguishable from zero, even though KD taught it language (held-out perplexity 477, against the converged arms' 44–95). distilgpt2, a real published distillation, retains only about 60% of its teacher's alignment above the floor ($\Delta=+0.012$, $p=0.001$), but that 0.60 is an upper bound measured against its own teacher (gpt2), not gpt2-medium [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The rank order is stable across PCA rank (it holds at $n_{\text{pca}}\in\{25,50,100\}$), and the references behave: off-the-shelf gpt2 sits at the teacher's level ($\rho'\approx1.0$), the untrained control at the floor, and two parallel runs scoring the same references on different GPUs reproduced them to ~0.0003 [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). So the kill-test did not return preserve-by-default.

The over-reach the post-run adversarial refutation caught: **alignment co-varies with language-model quality**, so the gradient cannot be attributed to the distillation objective on this evidence (L011). Across the five trained and distilled points, alignment is tightly predicted by log-perplexity at Pearson $r=-0.88$; the fit $\text{align} = 0.050 - 0.0079\,\ln(\text{ppl})$ places warm-init KD essentially *on* the line (residual +0.0001) and leaves conventional gpt2 as the lone outlier above it (+0.006) [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The two dissociations that would separate objective-specific shedding from "alignment tracks quality" are both only marginal: warm-init KD has lower alignment than gpt2 despite better perplexity, but the bootstrap $P(\text{gpt2}\le\text{kd\_warm})=0.092$; and at matched budget the no-teacher finetune beats warm-init KD on both perplexity and alignment, but $P(\text{lmft}\le\text{kd\_warm})=0.085$. Both lean on a single unreplicated gpt2 point and, under a paired-fold test, on one fold carrying about 44% of the signal [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The from-scratch arm compounds this with under-training: its held-out perplexity (477) sits far above the converged arms' (warm-init KD 95, plain finetune 44), so its near-floor alignment partly just reflects a worse language model, the convergence-guard caveat the design predeclared [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

A real caveat on magnitudes: the absolute unique R² at the verdict layer declines with PCA rank for every model, and the two confounded arms flip sign: cold-init KD reads +0.009 / −0.004 / −0.010 and distilgpt2 +0.017 / +0.008 / −0.004 at $n_{\text{pca}}=25/50/100$ [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). The *ordering* of the gradient is preserved at every rank, but the quoted shed fractions should be read as rank-dependent, not exact [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

## The verdict

Plain perplexity-only knowledge distillation does not preserve brain alignment by default: a from-scratch logit-KD student lands far below its teacher ($\Delta=+0.018$, $p<0.001$, $\rho'=0.37$ [0.14, 0.58]), and alignment falls along a monotone gradient, stable across PCA rank, that tracks how far each model's representation was rebuilt away from conventional pretraining [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). By the predeclared decision rule the cold-init arm sits in the partial-headroom band ($0.33 < \rho' < 0.80$), bordering the large-headroom band, so the kill-test cleanly rules out the preserve-for-free outcome: perplexity-only KD leaves genuine alignment headroom below the teacher. That headroom co-varies with the perplexity each arm pays ($r=-0.88$), so the gradient cannot be read as the distillation objective shedding alignment; whether an alignment-guided objective could recover the headroom is the next question, not one this gradient settles.

What it does not license is "the distillation objective sheds alignment beyond the quality it costs." That stronger, load-bearing claim is unsupported at this benchmark: the headroom co-varies with perplexity ($r=-0.88$), the objective-specific dissociations are only $p\approx0.09$, and the verdict arm is under-trained [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md) [L011](../learnings.md). So Q1 is neither a non-problem (plain KD plainly does not preserve) nor a confirmation that an alignment-guided objective has a job. The question that decides that, whether a brain-alignment objective recovers alignment *beyond* what the perplexity objective already implies, is the matched-perplexity question, and it is answered in its own reports (the lever, then the per-individual null), not pre-judged here. The discipline R06 fixed carries forward: real ≠ shed-by-objective; this report establishes only that plain KD sheds, not why.

## The caveats (carried in the claim, not a footnote)

- The benchmark is the Tuckute ROI screen: five dimensions, train-participant-averaged, NC $\approx 0.35$. It is adequate as a cheap screen and is *not* powered to resolve a real ~0.005 objective-specific gap; that resolution moves to the LeBel voxelwise substrate (R06's E006 apparatus), where it is run under matched perplexity rather than matched budget [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md) [L011](../learnings.md).
- The verdict arm is under-trained (its perplexity, 477, is far above the converged arms' 44–95), so part of its near-floor alignment is "a worse language model," not "distillation shed alignment." A clean attribution needs a cold student trained to matched perplexity, which is exactly the design the matched-perplexity reports adopt [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md) [L011](../learnings.md).
- distilgpt2 is context, not verdict: it carries a capacity confound (82M / 6L versus 124M / 12L) and its training included a hidden-state cosine term, so it is not pure perplexity-only KD and its retention is an upper bound, friendlier to preservation than a pure-logit student would be [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).
- The shed *fractions* are PCA-rank-sensitive (the two confounded arms flip sign across $n_{\text{pca}}=25/50/100$); only the gradient's *ordering* is stable across rank. Read the ordering as the finding and the magnitudes as rank-dependent [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).
- "Preserve" and "shed" are claims about this ROI screen at a matched corpus and compute budget (and, as noted above, the cold arm in fact ran twice the epochs, so even the step count was not matched). The thesis-deciding form of the question is at matched *perplexity* on the powered substrate, and that is where Q2 and Q3 take it.
