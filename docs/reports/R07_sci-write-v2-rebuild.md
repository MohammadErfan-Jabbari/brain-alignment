# Perplexity-only knowledge distillation does not preserve brain alignment for free - but at this screen the loss cannot be separated from language-model quality

> [!NOTE]

It binds to the same frozen evidence ([E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md)) but its message, claims, skeleton, and prose were derived independently (lattice → F16 gate → draft → 4 audit/revise rounds to reader convergence). Math is rendered to LaTeX at the container layer; the pipeline drafts in ASCII. It is **not** wired into the canonical `reports/AGENTS.md` ordering.

Knowledge distillation is designed to transfer what a teacher learned, so a common prior is that a distilled student keeps the teacher's brain alignment - the linear predictability of fMRI voxel responses from a model's layer activations. A GPT-2 student trained from a cold (random) start on only the teacher's next-token logits does not keep it: its alignment drops far below the teacher ($\Delta=+0.018$, $p<0.001$), down to a floor-anchored retention of $\rho'=0.37$ (0 is the untrained floor, 1 is teacher-grade) whose raw value is not distinguishable from zero - so the certified result is the drop from the teacher, and 0.37 is the upper read of a near-floor retention, not a securely-kept third [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). That drop is real but confounded: this cold student reached only held-out perplexity 477 against the teacher's 74, so part of its near-floor alignment reflects a weaker language model rather than anything the distillation objective specifically shed, and across arms alignment co-varies tightly with language-model quality at Pearson $r=-0.88$; the dissociations that would separate objective-specific shedding from general quality are only marginal ($p\approx0.09$), so this screen does not resolve whether the objective sheds alignment beyond the quality it costs [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md). Plain perplexity-only KD does not preserve brain alignment for free; how much of the loss the objective itself drives, rather than the quality the student sheds, is unresolved at this benchmark.

## The question and the empty cell

A common prior holds that compressing a model can only cost alignment, and that the cost is a matter of capacity rather than training objective. For fixed-function compression - quantization or pruning, which perturb a network the teacher already trained - this prior follows from the data-processing inequality: it bounds the student's information about the stimulus by the teacher's, so such a student can lose alignment but not create it. Knowledge distillation is not subject to that bound, because a student fit from scratch on the teacher's logits is not a perturbed copy of the teacher; it has a stimulus side-channel through the raw next-token targets that bypasses the teacher's features entirely, making whether distillation preserves alignment an empirical question rather than a corollary of the compression bound. The from-scratch stimulus side-channel follows from the mechanism; how much alignment it transmits is not quantifiable at this screen, which is confounded by language-model quality and coarse in ROI resolution.

## The design and the statistic

We use a cold-init (randomly initialized) student as the verdict arm rather than a warm-started one, because a warm-started student begins at full teacher-grade alignment - prior work on this same screen puts a pretrained GPT-2 at a brain-alignment score (unique encoding $R^2$) of about $+0.020$, essentially the teacher's own level ($+0.0188$) - so its high retention would only measure fine-tuning drift from that inherited alignment, not what the distillation channel itself transmits. The cold-init arm is the only one whose ending alignment was transmitted through the KD channel rather than inherited from pretraining; the warm-init KD arm is kept as a labelled fine-tuning-drift control rather than a verdict arm. We match arms on corpus, batch size, learning rate, and architecture, but not optimizer-step count: the cold arm ran roughly twice the warm arms' steps (2 epochs versus 1), so "matched budget" means matched corpus and compute-shape, not matched steps. This rationale rests on a single warm-start measurement standing in for "warm-init begins at full teacher-grade alignment" - an unreplicated point at this same coarse screen rather than a swept curve.

We build the measurement so that capacity, leakage, and noise-floor artifacts do not inflate or suppress the alignment estimate, leaving language-model quality as the one confound the design does not remove (the next section takes that up). We summarize each arm with a floor-anchored retention statistic, $\rho' = (A - A_0) / (A_T - A_0)$, where $A$ is the arm's alignment, $A_0$ the untrained floor, and $A_T$ the teacher's; we prefer it over the raw teacher ratio because that ratio is ill-conditioned near the decision cutoffs (its denominator is $\approx 0.019$ against the floor-anchored $\approx 0.029$) and because $\rho'=0$ correctly maps to "fell back to the floor." We measure alignment as noise-ceiling-normalized unique encoding $R^2$: the variance an encoding model (the linear map from activations to voxel responses) explains after a capacity-fair nuisance subtraction for sentence length, position, and static embeddings, divided by the noise ceiling - the reliability-set upper bound on $R^2$, here NC $\approx 0.353$, a property of the data, not of any model. The screen covers the five left-hemisphere language ROIs of the Tuckute 2024 stimulus set, scored under contiguous-block cross-validation - a leakage-safe temporal split that keeps adjacent stimuli from straddling the train/test boundary - at a fixed layer (L7 for the 12-layer GPT-2 arms) over three seeds, with bootstrap confidence intervals over folds and seeds.

## The evidence

The results table gives each arm. Each row reports the noise-ceiling-normalized unique $R^2$, the floor-anchored retention $\rho'$ with its bootstrap 95% CI, the gap $\Delta$ against the teacher, the bootstrap probability of no drop from the teacher, and the held-out perplexity.

| Arm | init / objective | unique $R^2$ | NC-norm | $\rho'$ [95% CI] | $\Delta$ vs teacher | $p$(no-drop) | ppl |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| teacher gpt2-medium | pretrained | +0.0188 | 0.053 | - | - | - | 74 |
| gpt2 | pretrained (conventional) | +0.0195 | 0.055 | 1.02 [0.77, 1.27] | $\approx 0$ | 0.57 | 105 |
| lmft_warm | warm + plain LM finetune | +0.0174 | 0.049 | 0.95 [0.81, 1.13] | +0.0016 | 0.265 | 44 |
| kd_warm | warm + pure logit KD | +0.0144 | 0.041 | 0.84 [0.67, 1.04] | +0.0046 | 0.061 | 95 |
| distilgpt2 | real distillation (6L/82M, +hidden-cosine) | +0.0076 | 0.021 | 0.60 [0.42, 0.84] | +0.0119 | 0.001 | 169 |
| kd_cold | random + pure logit KD | +0.0005 | 0.002 | 0.37 [0.14, 0.58] | +0.0181 | 0.000 | 477 |
| untrained | random (floor) | −0.0102 | −0.029 | 0 | - | - | $\approx 6\times10^4$ |

**Table 1.** Per-arm brain alignment on the Tuckute ROI screen; the cold-init logit-KD verdict arm sits at $\rho'=0.37$ [0.14, 0.58], $\Delta=+0.018$ ($p<0.001$). The distilgpt2 retention is anchored to its own GPT-2 teacher, not gpt2-medium.

Across arms, retention falls in a monotone ordering that tracks how much of each model's representation was rebuilt from the teacher's outputs rather than inherited from ordinary pretraining: conventional gpt2 ($\rho'\approx1.0$) > warm-init KD (0.84) > distilgpt2 (0.60) > cold-init KD (0.37) > the untrained floor (0), and this rank-ordering is stable across PCA rank. The ordering is descriptive, not causal: the same ranking is equally well explained by language-model quality, with which rebuild-extent is collinear, so we do not claim that representation-rebuilding drives alignment independently of quality. This collinearity is the confound the screen cannot untangle: across the five trained and distilled points, held-out log-perplexity predicts alignment tightly (Pearson $r=-0.88$), the two objective-specific dissociations that would separate KD-specific shedding from general LM quality are only marginal ($p\approx0.09$), and the verdict arm is under-trained (held-out perplexity 477 against the teacher's 74), so whether the distillation objective sheds alignment beyond the quality it costs is not established at this benchmark [E003](../experiments/E003_kd-alignment-preservation.md).

We restrict the ordering claim to rank rather than to absolute magnitudes, because those magnitudes are PCA-rank-sensitive: the two confounded arms sign-flip across PCA rank, with kd_cold at $+0.009 / -0.004 / -0.010$ and distilgpt2 at $+0.017 / +0.008 / -0.004$ unique $R^2$ at $n_{\text{pca}} = 25 / 50 / 100$, while the arm ordering is preserved at every rank. The distilgpt2 arm is also biased toward higher retention, since it carries a capacity confound (82M parameters / 6 layers versus 124M / 12) and a hidden-state cosine term in its training, and is scored against its own GPT-2 teacher rather than gpt2-medium. The rank-ordering is therefore the only claim stable across this screen; the absolute magnitudes cannot be compared across arms.

Across the three PCA ranks ($n_{\text{pca}} = 25, 50, 100$), retention $\rho'$ per arm shows that the monotone ordering conventional gpt2 > warm-init KD > distilgpt2 > cold-init KD > untrained floor holds at every rank, even as the absolute unique-$R^2$ magnitudes shrink with rank and the two confounded arms (cold-init KD, distilgpt2) cross zero. The ordering is stable; the absolute magnitudes are not.

Plotting alignment against held-out log-perplexity exposes the confound directly: noise-ceiling-normalized unique $R^2$ against held-out log-perplexity for the five trained and distilled arms. The five points fall on a tight negative relation (Pearson $r=-0.88$), with the fitted line $\text{align} = 0.050 - 0.0079\ln(\text{ppl})$. Warm-init KD falls essentially on that line (residual $+0.0001$), and conventional gpt2 is the lone positive outlier above it ($+0.006$). The two objective-specific dissociations are both marginal under a fold-by-seed bootstrap: kd_warm has lower alignment than gpt2 despite better perplexity at $P(\text{gpt2} \le \text{kd\_warm})=0.092$, and at matched budget lmft_warm beats kd_warm on both perplexity and alignment at $P(\text{lmft} \le \text{kd\_warm})=0.085$. This non-result is itself limited by the screen's power and by construction-sensitivity: the two $P$-values flip to $\approx 0.00$ under a fold-only bootstrap, lean on a single unreplicated gpt2 point and one fold carrying $\approx 44\%$ of the signal, and the coarse ROI screen (5 dimensions, NC $\approx 0.35$) is not powered to resolve a real $\approx 0.005$ objective-specific gap, so the objective's effect is undemonstrated here, not shown to be zero.

## What it means

Plain perplexity-only KD does not preserve brain alignment for free: full retention would have left an alignment-guided objective with nothing to recover, and at $\rho'=0.37$ the cold student sits well below it. The alignment gap we measure is the headroom an alignment-guided objective could in principle recover, yet this screen does not establish that such an objective improves on matched-quality training; separating the objective's contribution from the quality difference requires a matched-perplexity comparison deferred to later work. The objective-versus-quality question is unresolved at this benchmark itself, not merely pending future work: the measured headroom co-varies tightly with log-perplexity ($r=-0.88$) and the dissociation needed to separate the two is only marginal here, so this screen establishes "headroom exists" while "an alignment-guided objective improves on a matched-perplexity baseline" remains undemonstrated. Because the Tuckute ROI screen serves only as a cheap screen, the direct next step is a cold arm trained to matched perplexity rather than matched budget, with confirmation on a powered voxelwise benchmark able to resolve a $\approx 0.005$ objective-specific gap.
