# R07 — does compressing a model keep its brain alignment?

**The plain-language version.** The thesis wants to use one number — how well a language model's middle layers
line up with brain activity ("brain alignment") — as a usable signal. The first real use case: knowledge
distillation, where you train a small "student" model to copy a big "teacher" model. R07 asks the question that
has to be answered *before* any of that has a point: when you distill a teacher into a smaller student, does
the student keep the teacher's brain alignment, or lose it?

The answer is a careful **PARTIAL** [R07]. Plain distillation does **not** keep alignment for free — a student
rebuilt from scratch against the teacher's outputs lands far below the teacher, keeping only a fraction of the
teacher's alignment [R07, E003]. But the report stops short of the stronger claim it would have liked to make.
The catch: alignment turns out to track plain language-model *quality* very tightly, so the report cannot prove
the *distillation objective itself* is what sheds alignment, rather than the student just being a worse language
model [R07, L011]. That gap between "KD sheds alignment" (shown) and "the KD objective sheds alignment beyond
the quality it costs" (not shown) is the whole subtlety of R07, and it is what makes the verdict PARTIAL.

Why it matters: if plain distillation already kept alignment, the thesis's central cell — *can an
alignment-guided objective recover alignment that plain compression loses?* — would be empty. R07 shows the
cell is open: there is real headroom below the teacher to try to recover [R07].
