## Comprehension Summary [REQUIRED]

1. **Problem solved:** Today's generative AI systems are "tuned to present information by default
   rather than engage users in service of learning as a human tutor would." LearnLM is Google's
   attempt to make a Gemini variant behave like a tutor — scaffolding and questioning rather than
   answer-dumping — without committing the model to one fixed theory of pedagogy.
2. **Core insight:** *Pedagogical instruction following* — "we contextualize training and evaluation
   examples with system-level instructions that describe desired pedagogical behavior." Pedagogy is
   treated as a controllable, instructable axis (a teacher/developer supplies the desired behavior in
   the system prompt) rather than a hard-wired single doctrine. The behaviors are learned via SFT then
   RLHF and *co-trained* into the general Gemini post-training mixture so general capability is not
   lost.
3. **If-wrong breakage:** The headline claim is a *human-preference* result (expert pedagogy raters
   prefer LearnLM's tutoring). If those preferences are confounded by style/verbosity rather than
   genuine pedagogical quality, the "better tutor" claim weakens. There is NO measured learning-outcome
   experiment (no test-score gain, no controlled trial of student learning) — the evidence is rated
   conversation quality, not learning.
4. **Main result location:** Preference deltas in §4 (Figures 4–7); rubric in Appendix B.6 / Table 8;
   training pipeline in §3; the "we avoid committing to one pedagogy" framing in §1; the
   "broadly agreed upon and evidence-based" caveat in the Conclusion (§5).

## Source Grounding

**The model.** LearnLM is Gemini 1.5 Pro adapted by post-training for pedagogy. Baselines compared:
GPT-4o (2024-08-06), Claude 3.5 Sonnet (2024-06-20), and the unmodified Gemini 1.5 Pro (2024-09-24).

**The evaluation corpus.** Tutoring *scenarios* were authored, each specifying subject area, subtopic,
setting, learning goal, grounding material, a learner persona, a conversation plan, an initial query,
and the system instructions. Roughly 49 core academic-subject scenarios plus ~50 medical-education
scenarios. Conversations were generated by **human pedagogy experts role-playing the learner** against
each model (not an LLM-simulated student). Participants were required to continue for **a minimum of 10
conversational turns (≥5 learner + ≥5 system turns)** before ending — and on average conversed for
about twice that. Totals: **2,360 conversations** collected and **10,192 expert pedagogical
assessments** (≈3 independent assessments per targeted conversation pair).

**The raters.** Pedagogy experts with advanced degrees and 2+ years tutoring experience — one pool
(~168–186) role-played learners to generate the conversations, and a pool (~228) rated transcripts.
Ratings used a **7-point Likert scale** (mapped to −3…+3) with a "Not applicable" option. Aggregation
used **Bayesian hierarchical models** to handle repeated measurements and non-independence; **95%
credible intervals** are reported and **no p-values** are used.

**The pedagogy rubric.** ~29 items grouped under five dimensions plus an overall-quality block.
The dimensions correspond to the named principles (see Key Claims C1) and contain items such as
(verbatim): "The tutor does not give away answers too quickly." · "The tutor asks questions to
encourage the student to think." · "The tutor uses bullet points and other formatting to break
information down into smaller, manageable chunks." · "The tutor tries to stimulate the student's
interest and curiosity." · "Overall, the tutor adapts to the student's needs." · "The tutor guides the
student to discover their own mistakes." The overall block includes the benchmark item that the tutor
is "as good as a very good human tutor."

**Training method.** A three-stage post-training pipeline: (1) **SFT** on pedagogical conversations
where "each conversation begins with a different System Instruction that specifically describes the
pedagogical behavior present in that conversation"; (2) a **reward model** trained on human preference
labels (turn-level and conversation-level), each conversation "seeded with a pedagogically-focused
System Instruction"; (3) **RLHF**. The behaviors are instilled by **co-training** — "mix pedagogical
conversation data alongside data that contains more typical interactions by conditioning pedagogical
model responses on specific System Instructions … by co-training with Gemini's post-training
mixture." Reported takeaway: "While SFT seems to improve pedagogical instruction following somewhat,
RL is significantly more effective."

## Core Claims

- `C1` (the named principles — what our /teach skill grounds on): LearnLM's pedagogy rubric is built
  around five learning-science dimensions. As they surface in the rubric and the §4 results figure,
  they are: **manage cognitive load** (response length, breaking content into "smaller, manageable
  chunks," clarity, logical order); **inspire active learning** (engagement opportunities, asking
  questions, "does not give away answers too quickly," guiding the learner to act rather than receive);
  **deepen metacognition** (guide the student "to discover their own mistakes," constructive feedback,
  communicate a clear plan); **stimulate curiosity** ("tries to stimulate the student's interest and
  curiosity," responds to frustration, encouraging feedback); and **adapt to the learner** (level to
  the student, help when stuck, proactive guidance, "does not withhold information unproductively").
  The paper itself frames these as "broadly agreed upon and evidence-based" core principles.
- `C2` (instructable, not doctrinaire): The model is NOT committed to one theory of teaching.
  Verbatim: "This framing avoids committing our models to any particular definition of pedagogy, and
  instead allows teachers or developers to specify desired model behavior." Hard constraints ("do not
  give away the answer") and soft preferences ("use a motivating tone") are both expressed as system
  instructions.
- `C3` (the headline empirical result): Across expert pedagogy-rater preferences, LearnLM is preferred
  over the baselines by **+31% over GPT-4o**, **+11% over Claude 3.5 Sonnet**, and **+13% over
  Gemini 1.5 Pro** (mean preference strength on the −3…+3 Likert scale; 95% credible intervals in
  figures). It rated highest across the rubric items, with standout margins on "inspiring active
  learning, deepening metacognition, and stimulating curiosity."
- `C4` (RL beats SFT for pedagogy): SFT moves pedagogical instruction-following "somewhat"; RL
  (RLHF on the pedagogy reward model) is "significantly more effective."

## Assumptions and Limits

The evidence is **rated conversation quality, not measured learning.** There is no controlled study of
student learning outcomes — no pre/post test gain, no randomized trial. "Better tutor" means "experts
prefer the tutoring behavior," which can be confounded with style (verbosity, tone, formatting), the
exact confound the rubric tries but cannot fully rule out. The learner side is **role-played by
experts**, not real students with real knowledge gaps, so engagement and frustration are simulated.
The baselines are zero-/light-prompted off-the-shelf models; a baseline given an equally detailed
pedagogy system prompt is not the comparison. Statistics are Bayesian preference intervals, not
significance tests, so "preferred by X%" is a preference-strength summary, not an effect size on any
learning metric. The principles are asserted as "broadly agreed upon," with the paper explicitly
declining to defend a single pedagogical theory.

## Interpretation Notes

**This is NOT a brain-science paper.** It touches none of A1/A2/A3, no brain-alignment number, no
dataset of ours. Its only role in this repo is as the **pedagogical grounding for the agent's own
`/teach` stance skill** — the source we cite when the agent claims its teaching behavior follows
learning science. Keep it filed as canonical for traceability, but it is orthogonal to the thesis
science and should not appear in [`01-research-landscape.md`](../../01-research-landscape.md)'s brain/distillation tables.

**The five named principles — cite them as LearnLM dimensions, with the right wording.** The popular
shorthand "inspire active learning / manage cognitive load / adapt to the learner / stimulate
curiosity / deepen metacognition" is faithful to LearnLM's five rubric dimensions and to its results
figure, and the paper does call these core principles "broadly agreed upon and evidence-based." But the
paper does **not** print them as a tidy five-item manifesto in the intro — they live in the rubric
(Appendix B.6) and the §4 figure labels. So our skill may cite "the five LearnLM pedagogy dimensions"
and quote the rubric items verbatim; it should NOT imply the paper opens with a numbered list of five
principles.

**"Guide, don't tell" — cite carefully (cite-or-flag).** The *spirit* is squarely LearnLM's: it
contrasts presenting information "by default" with engaging the learner, frames learning as "a process
of discovery rather than simply a transfer of information," and the rubric rewards "does not give away
answers too quickly" and "asks questions to encourage the student to think." But the exact slogan
"guide, don't tell" does **not** appear as a phrase in the paper. Our skill can attribute the
*scaffolding-and-questioning behavior* and the *discovery-over-transfer* framing to LearnLM verbatim;
it should treat "guide don't tell" as our own gloss, not a LearnLM quote.

**Three explicit absences our /teach skill MUST honor (the cite-or-flag guardrail):**

1. **Bloom's "two-sigma problem" is NOT in this paper.** No mention of Bloom, "two sigma," or the
   2σ tutoring effect anywhere in the text. If the skill invokes the two-sigma motivation, it must
   cite [Bloom (1984)](bloom-1984_two-sigma-problem.md) directly — NOT LearnLM — or flag it as background, never attribute it here.
2. **No turn-by-turn pacing rule.** The paper specifies only a *minimum conversation length* (≥10
   turns, ≥5 learner + ≥5 system) for data collection. It does NOT mandate one-question-per-turn or
   any per-turn structure. The "ask one question at a time / one question per turn" rule that shows up
   in Gemini/LearnLM *product* system prompts is a **product-prompt convention, not a LearnLM paper
   claim.** Our skill must not cite the paper for one-question-per-turn pacing; cite it as a product
   prompting convention or our own design choice.
3. **No learning-outcome / RCT evidence.** The skill must not claim LearnLM "improves learning
   outcomes" or "raises test scores." The paper shows *expert preference* for the tutoring behavior,
   full stop. Claims about measured learning gains need a different source (e.g., a real outcome study)
   or must be flagged as unestablished.

**Net for the skill:** cite LearnLM for (a) the five pedagogy dimensions and their verbatim rubric
items, (b) the instructable / discovery-over-transfer framing, (c) the empirical fact that an expert
panel preferred a tutor trained to these behaviors over GPT-4o/Claude/base-Gemini. Do NOT cite it for
Bloom's two-sigma, for one-question-per-turn pacing, or for any measured learning-outcome gain.

paper never measures?
