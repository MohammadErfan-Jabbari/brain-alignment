## Comprehension Summary [REQUIRED]

1. Problem solved: tests whether adding associative-memory content to language model inputs or training targets can improve model-to-brain alignment during natural story listening.
2. Core insight: targeted, word-level association cues and association-focused instruction tuning improve alignment most in regions tied to memory, language, and working memory.
3. If-wrong breakage: if gains come from generic augmentation noise rather than associative content, the paper's proposed mechanism for better brain alignment does not hold.

## Source Grounding

The paper builds a language-model-to-fMRI mapping pipeline (ridge regression over temporally aligned hidden states), then perturbs model inputs with simulated associative memory and measures score changes by voxel and ROI. It evaluates GPT-2 and LLaMA-2 on the Narratives dataset, adds control augmentations, and then tests whether supervised fine-tuning on a new 1,000-sample Association instruction dataset can further improve alignment.

## Core Claims

- `C1`: LLaMA-2 reaches higher peak brain alignment than GPT-2, and best-predictive layers are mid-depth rather than final layers.
- `C2`: Associative-memory augmentation improves alignment in distributed cortical regions, with word-level augmentation outperforming sentence-level augmentation and random augmentation controls.
- `C3`: Association-guided supervised fine-tuning yields additional ROI-specific gains, including measurable improvements in memory-related temporal regions.

## Evidence Pointers

- `C1` evidence: Section 5.1 (page 6), Figure 3; reported peak values include GPT-2 average peak around 0.126 (layer 9) and LLaMA-2 peaks around 0.146 (left hemisphere) and 0.135 (right hemisphere, layer 14).
- `C2` evidence: Section 5.2 (page 7), Figure 4 and Figure 5; reported ROI gain range is about 0.0014-0.02 in broad regions and up to about 0.05 in ROI analysis, with random augmentation showing none or negative growth.
- `C3` evidence: Section 5.3 (page 8), Figure 6 and Figure 7; reported gains include roughly 2%-7% in MTL-related regions after fine-tuning, with subject-level ROI analysis showing larger effects in superior parietal lobule (~50%-60% range). Association dataset size and construction are described in Section 3.4 (page 5) and Appendix A.3/Table 1-2 (pages 12-14).

## Assumptions and Limits

The study depends on one main narrative-listening fMRI corpus and English stimuli, so generalization across languages and tasks is not established. Simulated associative content is generated by humans or GPT-4 and then injected post hoc, so it is a proxy for real-time human memory retrieval rather than a direct measurement of participant associations.

## Interpretation Notes

The strongest gains come from compact lexical associations, not long sentence inserts, which suggests alignment is sensitive to targeted conceptual cues rather than extra fluent text. The control condition result (random augmentation not helping) supports a content-specific effect, though causal interpretation still depends on this mapping setup and dataset.

## Open Questions

- Do the gains persist with stricter out-of-distribution stories and subject splits?
- How much of the effect is specific to Narratives preprocessing choices and FIR settings?
- Can association-aware tuning improve alignment without hurting downstream language quality?
