# Errorful learning & learner models — `/teach` pedagogy synthesis

**Type:** synthesis / index over the per-paper canonical notes listed below. **Apparatus grounding for the
`/teach` stance, NOT thesis science** — touches no A1/A2/A3, produces no brain-alignment number. Keep it out
of `01-research-landscape.md`; never import a number here into a finding-report or manuscript.

## Bottom line

A visible record of a learner's own mistakes **helps an expert adult IFF** it is **diagnostic** ("what to
re-check next"), **coupled to re-retrieval**, and **selective** — and **harms** when it functions as a
**scorecard** / ability signal, is **exhaustive**, or is read through a **fixed mindset**. This is the
evidence base for `/teach`'s rule that mistakes live as diagnostic, forward-looking, supersede-on-correction
notes (across the lesson and records layers), never an accumulating tally — and for the SCR
predict-before-reveal loop.

## The evidence (each digested in its own canonical note)

- **Deliberate self-errors help** — `wong-lim-2022_derring-effect.md`. Committing then correcting one's
  *own* error beats errorless study on recall and transfer; specific to self-generated errors; learners are
  metacognitively unaware of the benefit, so it needs scaffolding (predict-before-reveal supplies it).
- **High-confidence errors are corrected best** — `butterfield-metcalfe-2001_hypercorrection-effect.md`.
  The hypercorrection effect: surprise at a confident error drives attention to the correction. Grounds
  surfacing confident mistakes and re-testing them.
- **Error framing is the moderator** — `moser-2011_growth-mindset-error-processing.md`. Growth-oriented
  processing (larger Pe / error-positivity) predicts better post-error accuracy; deficit framing suppresses
  it. Why teach's notes are diagnostic, not evaluative.
- **Exposing the learner model helps, conditionally** — `open-learner-models_2025-review.md` (Robles Mucho
  et al. 2025 meta-synthesis). Inspectable open learner models raise engagement and calibration, but under
  diagnostic framing and with learner control; visualization alone left most learners still overestimating.
- **Also relevant (not separately digested):** Tulis et al. (2024, *BJEP*) — point-of-error adaptive prompts
  beat background mindset messaging (the timing of the framing matters, not a standing orientation).

## Relevance to the `/teach` stance

Grounds the "diagnostic, not scorecard" mistake handling, the two-layer memory (lessons = diagnostic
process; records = mastery, supersede-on-correction), and the SCR predict-before-reveal loop. The
expert-adult learner is the favorable case — productive failure needs prior knowledge
(`bjork-2011_desirable-difficulties.md`).
