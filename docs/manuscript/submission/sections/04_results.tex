\subsection{Controlled brain predictivity under regional and naturalistic assays}
\label{sec:results-signal}

Trained representations exceeded the declared nuisance baseline in the controlled regional predictivity assay.
For the primary Qwen2.5-0.5B layer-12 comparison, controlled unique \(R^2\) was \result{e002_qwen_unique}, where the \(\pm\) term is the descriptive standard deviation across five contiguous stimulus folds.
The historical trained-minus-untrained difference was \result{e002_qwen_gap}, but the implementation recomputed the static nuisance block for each model.
That gap therefore does not cleanly isolate learned parameters against one fixed nuisance design.
Because the response target was averaged across five participants before readout fitting, the folds describe stimulus-block variation and do not support participant-level inference.
The assay therefore supports controlled regional predictivity for this fixed participant-averaged target.\evd{E002}

The controlled naturalistic voxelwise predictivity assay also favored trained representations when complete stories were held out.
For UTS03, the mean differences were \result{e006_gpt2_gap} for GPT-2 Small and \result{e006_qwen_gap} for Qwen2.5-0.5B, and all five held-out-story blocks had positive differences.
The voxels are spatially dependent, and the story folds share training stories, so neither provides independent biological replication.
This assay therefore supports a within-participant controlled brain predictivity result conditional on UTS03 and the fixed naturalistic protocol.\evd{E006}

\subsection{Observed distillation headroom and its association with language quality}
\label{sec:results-headroom}

The from-scratch GPT-2 comparison contains an observed teacher-relative gap, but it does not establish quality-aware headroom for the intervention routes.
Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-retention} places the GPT-2 teacher, floor, and student conditions on the floor-anchored retention scale defined in Equation~\ref{eq:headroom}.
At the fixed Tuckute layers, the randomly initialized GPT-2 Small logit-\ac{kd} student finishes \result{e003_kd_cold_delta} below the GPT-2 Medium teacher in controlled brain predictivity.
Relative to the architecture-matched untrained GPT-2 Small floor, the student retains \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_point} of the teacher-to-floor interval.
The recorded 95\% bootstrap interval over folds and seeds is \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_ci}.\evd{E003}
The tested cold-start route therefore leaves an observed gap within this assay, although the comparison does not identify its source.

Language quality is a material competing explanation.
The randomly initialized student remains substantially weaker as an \ac{lm}, with perplexity \result{e003_kd_cold_ppl}, compared with \result{e003_teacher_ppl} for the teacher.\evd{E003}
The evaluated conditions also differ in initialization and training history, not only in objective.
Their ordered predictivity estimates establish a descriptive teacher--student gap, but they do not show that compression or \ac{kd} caused the gap.
The pretrained GPT-2 Small condition supplies closer initialization and language-quality context for the synthetic-response route, but the completed analysis did not establish a formal quality-matched headroom result for that warm route.
The recorded-response intervention uses Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-0.5B, for which route-matched teacher--student headroom was not measured.\evd{E009}

The broader model comparison shows why later interventions must control language quality.
Across 22 decoder-only \acp{lm} from six families, controlled brain predictivity is negatively associated with log bits per byte, where higher values indicate poorer language quality.\evd{E015}
Pearson \(r\) is \result{e015_full_corr}, with a family-cluster 95\% \ac{ci} of \result{e015_full_ci}.\evd{E015}
Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-quality} shows this full-range association and marks the capable-model band, defined as the \result{e015_capable_n} models with bits per byte below \result{e015_capable_bpb_max}.
Within that band, Pearson \(r\) is \result{e015_capable_corr}, with a 95\% \ac{ci} of \result{e015_capable_ci}.\evd{E015}
The point estimate remains moderately negative, but its interval includes zero.
Model family, scale, training data, and language quality remain entangled, so the association identifies language quality as a design constraint rather than a causal explanation.

\subsection{Recorded-response intervention at valid inference units}
\label{sec:results-recorded}

Participant-averaged recorded responses produce positive recorded-response-minus-permuted-response point estimates, but neither comparison establishes a reliable pairing benefit at the shared-fold inference unit.
In the initial loss-form screen, the mean contrast is \result{e004_qwen_specific}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e004_qwen_fold_ci}.\evd{E004}
In the \ac{kd}-anchored paired intervention, the mean contrast is \result{e005_apparent}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e005_fold_ci}.\evd{E005}
Both intervals include zero, and both estimates are sensitive to a dominant fold.
They therefore support positive averaged-target trends, not demonstrated pairing advantages.

These averaged-response comparisons answer a narrower question than participant-level inference.
Averaging responses before training creates one shared stimulus-locked target, so the fold-level contrast asks whether correct stimulus--response pairing helps for that fixed target.
It does not estimate the mean intervention effect across participants.
The recorded-response arm and permuted-response control have perplexities \result{e005_real_ppl} and \result{e005_permuted_ppl}, respectively, showing no gross quality imbalance without establishing a formal equivalence margin.\evd{E005}

The participant-count diagnostic does not produce a stable monotone relationship once random participant subsets replace the confounded nested sequence.\evd{E010}
With the student frozen, averaging nevertheless increases controlled brain predictivity by reducing response noise and emphasizing the shared response component; individual responses still contain measurable signal.\evd{E014}
Averaging can therefore strengthen measurement of the shared response without creating biological replication.

The participant-specific intervention does not demonstrate a participant-general advantage at the biological inference unit.
After the technical seed and fold repetitions are aggregated within each participant as defined in Section~\ref{sec:recorded-intervention}, the mean recorded-response-minus-permuted-response effect across nine participants is \result{e008_mean}, with participant \(t\) interval \result{e008_ci}.\evd{E008}
The shared-fold summary is also centered near zero at \result{e008_fold_mean}, with five-fold \(t\) interval \result{e008_fold_ci}.\evd{E008}

The observed-variance 80\%-power \ac{mde} for the participant analysis was approximately \result{e008_mde_range}.
This range is far below the earlier participant-averaged point estimate of \result{e005_apparent}.
An individual-level effect of that earlier magnitude would therefore have been detectable under the participant analysis.
The comparison does not equate the two results because they use different response constructions, scales, and estimands.
The participant result neither proves an exactly zero effect nor extends beyond the tested target, model, cohort, objective, and readout.\evd{E005}\evd{E008}

\subsection{Measurability of the 50-component linear target projection}
\label{sec:results-predicted-identification}

This assay asks whether the 50-component \ac{pca} representation of the TRIBE target adds held-out linear predictivity of recorded \ac{fmri} responses beyond the nuisance model.
Section~\ref{sec:measurement-prerequisites} defines \(A(u)\) as this unique increment for participant \(u\).
It defines \(Q(u)\) as the additional value of correct sentence--target pairing over the frozen row-twin control.
Both quantities are aggregated across folds within each participant, making the participant the biological inference unit.

The aligned target does not pass the inherited continuation rule.
Let \(\bar{A}\) denote the mean of \(A(u)\) across the \result{e030_participant_n} participants, let \(U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})\) denote the upper endpoint of its family-adjusted interval, and let \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\) denote the inherited continuation threshold.
The result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{A}
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique}},
\\
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_ci}},
\\
U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_upper}}
<
\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}
=
\text{\result{e030_practical_threshold}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exact-target-measurability-result}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:exact-target-measurability-result} shows that even the family-adjusted upper endpoint remains below the inherited continuation threshold.
Only \result{e030_target_positive_n} participant effects are positive, and every participant- and block-leave-out mean remains negative.
The assay therefore supports an internal noncontinuation decision under its frozen conditions.
The reference does not define a universal threshold for biological importance. \evd{E030}

Correct sentence--target pairing remains unresolved.
The frozen row twin preserves the target rows and readout procedure while breaking their pairing with sentences.
The participant-mean aligned-minus-row-twin contrast is \result{e030_target_twin}.
Its family-95\% interval is \result{e030_target_twin_ci}.
\result{e030_target_twin_positive_n} participant contrasts are positive, but the interval spans zero.
The assay therefore does not establish that correct pairing adds predictive value over this frozen row-twin control. \evd{E030}

\subsection{Synthetic-response manipulation checks and attribution status}
\label{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation}

Training changed the saved student despite the negative result for the separate target-projection assay.
The first manipulation check asks whether a fresh readout recovers more of the synthetic target from the synthetic-response student than from its seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd} counterpart.
Let \(R^2_{s,a}(T)\) denote held-out WikiText target recovery for technical seed \(s\), training arm \(a\), and target \(T\).
Let \(S\) denote the number of saved technical seeds (\result{e016_seed_n}).
The mean uptake contrast is
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{uptake}}^{\mathrm{Wiki}}
=
\frac{1}{S}
\sum_{s=1}^{S}
\left[
R^2_{s,\mathrm{synthetic}}(T)
-
R^2_{s,\mathrm{KD}}(T)
\right].
\label{eq:results-wiki-uptake}
\end{equation}
Equation~\ref{eq:results-wiki-uptake} measures the increase in linear target recoverability after synthetic-response training relative to ordinary \ac{kd}.
Its estimate is \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain}, with two-sided 95\% seed-axis \(t\) interval \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain_ci}.
The saved procedure therefore passes the WikiText target-uptake check.
These seeds quantify technical stability, not biological or model-population uncertainty. \evd{E016}\evd{E025}

The retained student also moved while measured language quality remained within the declared margin.
Mean movement from the ordinary \ac{kd} student, measured by layer-6 centered relative Frobenius distance, is \result{e025_tribe_movement_mean}.
Every saved technical seed passes the movement check.
Across the direct saved-arm comparisons, the largest absolute language-quality difference is \result{e025_quality_max_gap} bits per byte, below the frozen \result{e025_quality_margin} margin.
These results show that the intervention changed the retained student without a detected language-quality discrepancy larger than the declared margin.
They do not show that brain-response content caused the change or improved language quality. \evd{E025}

Fresh recovery on the Tuckute sentences gives a narrower result.
Absolute target extractability from the synthetic-response students is \result{e030_target_extract}, with Bonferroni family-95\% interval \result{e030_target_extract_ci}.
The incremental synthetic-response-minus-ordinary \ac{kd} estimate is instead \result{e030_retention_delta}, with Bonferroni family-95\% interval \result{e030_retention_delta_ci}.
Thus, the target is linearly recoverable from the trained students on this new substrate, but the analysis does not establish that synthetic-response training increased its recoverability beyond ordinary \ac{kd}.
The \result{e030_seed_n} seeds again describe stability of the saved training procedure rather than biological replication. \evd{E030}

The manipulation results cannot be attributed to brain-response content because the available text-derived auxiliary control is not adequately matched.
The corrected comparator audit fails \result{e026_check_split} identification checks; because these are pre-outcome or baseline identification conditions, any required failure leaves the comparator non-identifying for the attribution claim.
Before intervention, ordinary \ac{kd} representations recover mean target \(R^2\) of \result{e026_kd_targets} for the synthetic and text-derived targets, respectively.
This leaves \result{e026_headroom_ratio} times as much remaining mean headroom for the synthetic target.
Required identification margins also fail for covariance geometry, effective rank, and low-level nuisance predictability.
Initial auxiliary-loss and gradient comparability remain unresolved because those quantities were not retained.
Separately, \result{e026_diagnostic_split} post-training diagnostic margins depart from their original engineering ranges.
Parameter displacement, centered-Frobenius movement, and \ac{cka} describe how the interventions affected the students; they do not determine comparator validity.
The pre-outcome and baseline mismatches can affect apparent learnability and retained-student movement without any difference in brain-response content.
The audit does not establish which mismatch caused the observed cross-target difference. \evd{E026}

\subsection{Saved-student biological-transfer assays}
\label{sec:results-predicted-transfer}

The preceding manipulation checks show that synthetic-response training changed the saved student, but they do not show that the change improves prediction of recorded brain responses.
The direct biological-transfer assay therefore compares each synthetic-response student with its seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd} student.
Folds and technical seeds are averaged within each participant, and the participant is the biological inference unit (\result{e025_participant_n}).
The participant-level result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{direct}}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd}},\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd_ci}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:results-direct-biological-transfer}
\end{equation}
Here, \(\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{direct}}\) is the participant-mean change in held-out unique \(R^2\) produced by synthetic-response training relative to ordinary \ac{kd}.
The interval spans zero, so the direct assay does not support participant-general biological transfer in the tested cohort.
It does not establish that the effect is exactly zero, and its scope remains limited to the fixed target, student, layer, nuisance design, sentences, participants, and \ac{roi} endpoint. \evd{E025}

The synthetic-response-versus-text-derived auxiliary-control contrast is numerically positive, but it does not overturn the direct result:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{synthetic-text}}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd}}
-
\left(\mbox{\result{e025_textfeat_kd}}\right)
=
\mbox{\result{e025_participant_delta}},\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_participant_ci}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:results-control-transfer-decomposition}
\end{equation}
This decomposition shows that the positive relative contrast arises because the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is more negative than ordinary \ac{kd}, not because the synthetic-response arm has a positive direct effect.
The interval also spans zero.
Section~\ref{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation} shows that the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is not adequately matched as a comparator.
The contrast therefore cannot identify an effect of brain-response content. \evd{E025}\evd{E026}

The composed-path diagnostic asks a different question.
It measures whether recorded-response predictivity can pass through the synthetic-target component recovered from a frozen student.
Figure~\ref{fig:saved-student-biological-transfer}b reports its three participant-level estimands:
\begin{align}
\widehat{O}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{O})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs_ci}},
\\
\widehat{D}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{D})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta_ci}},
\\
\widehat{J}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{J})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin_ci}}.
\end{align}
Here, \(O\) measures absolute aligned predictive overlap, \(D\) measures its increment over ordinary \ac{kd}, and \(J\) asks whether that increment is larger for correctly aligned targets than for the frozen row twin.
Every family-adjusted interval spans zero.
The composed route therefore does not demonstrate predictive overlap, incremental overlap over ordinary \ac{kd}, or an aligned-over-row-twin increment.
These quantities are predictive-path diagnostics, not causal-mediation estimates or replacements for the direct biological-transfer endpoint. \evd{E030}

\subsection{Practical-utility evaluation}
\label{sec:results-utility}

The final subsection returns to the recorded-response route, which contains the only completed practical-utility evaluation.
It compares the averaged recorded-response arm with its approximately quality-matched block-permuted-response control across \result{e009_seed_n} technical training seeds.
Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-ratio} defines the out-of-domain to in-domain perplexity ratio and the recorded-response-minus-permuted-response contrast, for which negative values favor the recorded-response arm.
Averaging that contrast across seeds gives
\begin{equation}
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{Pereira}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_pereira_recorded_permuted}},
\qquad
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{LeBel}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_lebel_recorded_permuted}}.
\label{eq:results-practical-utility}
\end{equation}
These are the mean contrasts for Pereira sentences~\autocite{pereira2018} and LeBel story text~\autocite{lebel2023}, respectively.
Both point estimates favor the recorded-response arm.
The completed analysis did not report paired technical-seed uncertainty or a formal equivalence test, and an \ac{mde} cannot substitute for either.
The endpoint contrasts are therefore inconclusive. \evd{E009}

Interpreting this downstream result requires a reliable difference in controlled brain predictivity between the two arms.
Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-prerequisite} defines this prerequisite contrast in controlled brain predictivity as \(A_s\).
Its seed-level summary is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{mean}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mean}},
\qquad
\operatorname{median}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_median}},
\qquad
\mathrm{MDE}_{80\%}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mde}}.
\label{eq:results-utility-prerequisite}
\end{equation}
The \ac{mde} in Equation~\ref{eq:results-utility-prerequisite} is a sensitivity reference for the prerequisite contrast, not an acceptance threshold or equivalence margin.
Only \result{e009_positive_seed_signs} contrasts are positive.
Seed~0 contributes \result{e009_seed0_gap}, whereas the other seven seeds average approximately \result{e009_other_seven_mean}.
The mixed signs and seed-0 influence do not establish a technically stable prerequisite change.
The completed evidence therefore does not identify the endpoint means as the utility of a reliably induced brain-response-specific representational change. \evd{E009}
