\subsection{Controlled brain predictivity under regional and naturalistic assays}
\label{sec:results-signal}

Trained representations exceeded the declared nuisance baseline in the controlled regional predictivity assay.
For the primary Qwen2.5-0.5B layer-12 comparison, controlled unique \(R^2\) was \result{e002_qwen_unique}, where \(\pm\) is the descriptive standard deviation across the five stimulus folds.
The recorded trained-minus-untrained difference is \result{e002_qwen_gap}, but the executed runner built the static nuisance block separately for each model.
Because the target is participant-averaged, the folds describe stimulus-block variation and do not support participant-level inference.
The assay therefore supports controlled regional predictivity for this fixed participant-averaged target.\evd{E002}

Trained representations also exceeded their matched untrained controls in the controlled naturalistic voxelwise predictivity assay when complete stories were held out.
For participant UTS03, the mean differences were \result{e006_gpt2_gap} for GPT-2 Small and \result{e006_qwen_gap} for Qwen2.5-0.5B, and all five held-out-story blocks had positive differences.
Voxels are spatially dependent and story folds share training stories, so neither provides independent biological replication.
This assay therefore supports a within-participant controlled brain predictivity result conditional on UTS03 and the fixed naturalistic~protocol.\evd{E006}

The assays do not identify a shared computation between the \acp{lm} and the brain or establish population-general controlled brain predictivity.

\subsection{Observed distillation headroom and its association with language quality}
\label{sec:results-headroom}

The from-scratch GPT-2 comparison shows an observed teacher-relative gap, not the quality-aware headroom the intervention routes need, which is a teacher--student gap measured at comparable language quality.
At the fixed Tuckute layers, the randomly initialized GPT-2 Small logit-\ac{kd} student is \result{e003_kd_cold_delta} below the GPT-2 Medium teacher in controlled brain predictivity (bootstrap probability that the student does not drop below the teacher, \(p\) = \result{e003_kd_cold_nodrop_p}, over the cold arm's folds and seeds).
Relative to the architecture-matched untrained GPT-2 Small floor, the student retains \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_point} of the teacher-to-floor interval (Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-retention}, Equation~\ref{eq:floor-anchored-retention}).
The recorded 95\% bootstrap interval over folds and seeds is \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_ci}.\evd{E003}

Language quality is a material competing explanation.
The randomly initialized student remains substantially weaker as an \ac{lm}, with perplexity \result{e003_kd_cold_ppl}, compared with \result{e003_teacher_ppl} for the teacher.\evd{E003}
The conditions also differ in initialization and training history, not only in objective, so the estimates do not show that compression or \ac{kd} caused the gap.
The pretrained GPT-2 Small condition, the warm route, is closer to the teacher in initialization and language quality and is the relevant reference for the synthetic-response route.
The recorded-response intervention uses Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-0.5B\evd{E009}, for which we did not measure route-matched teacher--student headroom.\evd{E003}\evd{E015}

Across \result{e015_full_n} decoder-only \acp{lm} from six families, participant-averaged controlled brain predictivity is negatively associated with log bits per byte, where higher values indicate poorer language quality.\evd{E015}
Pearson \(r\) is \result{e015_full_corr}, with a family-cluster 95\% \ac{ci} of \result{e015_full_ci}.\evd{E015}
Within the capable-model band, the \result{e015_capable_n} models with bits per byte below \result{e015_capable_bpb_max}, the association stays moderately negative but its interval includes zero (Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-quality}, Appendix~\ref{app:sensitivities}).\evd{E015}
Model family, scale, training data, and language quality remain entangled, so the association is a design constraint, not a causal explanation.

\subsection{Recorded-response intervention at valid inference units}
\label{sec:results-recorded}

Participant-averaged recorded responses produce positive recorded-response-minus-permuted-response point estimates at the shared-fold inference unit.
In the loss-form screen that selected the auxiliary response loss, the mean contrast is \result{e004_qwen_specific}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e004_qwen_fold_ci}.\evd{E004}
In the \ac{kd}-anchored paired intervention, the mean contrast is \result{e005_apparent}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e005_fold_ci}.\evd{E005}
The recorded-response arm and permuted-response control have perplexities \result{e005_real_ppl} and \result{e005_permuted_ppl}, respectively, showing no gross quality imbalance without establishing a formal equivalence margin.\evd{E005}
Both intervals include zero, and both estimates are sensitive to a dominant fold.
A pairing advantage is not demonstrated (Figure~\ref{fig:recorded-response-averaged}).

Averaging responses before training creates one shared stimulus-locked target, so the fold-level contrast asks whether correct stimulus--response pairing helps for that fixed target.
It does not estimate the mean intervention effect across participants.

The participant-count diagnostic, re-run with random subsets so that count no longer varies with participant identity, shows no stable monotone relationship between participant count and the contrast.\evd{E010}
With model representations held fixed, averaging increases controlled brain predictivity by reducing response noise and emphasizing the shared response component; individual responses still carry measurable signal, so averaging strengthens measurement of the shared response without creating biological replication.\evd{E014}

The participant-specific intervention does not demonstrate a participant-general advantage at the biological inference unit, the participant.
Aggregating seed and fold repetitions within each participant (Section~\ref{sec:recorded-intervention}), the mean recorded-response-minus-permuted-response effect across nine participants is \result{e008_mean}, with participant \(t\) interval \result{e008_ci}.\evd{E008}
The exact sign test over the same participants gives \result{e008_sign_positive_n} positive, \(p\) = \result{e008_sign_p}.
The shared-fold summary is also centered near zero at \result{e008_fold_mean}, with five-fold \(t\) interval \result{e008_fold_ci} (Figure~\ref{fig:recorded-response-participant}).\evd{E008}

The minimum detectable effect at 80\% power, computed from the observed variance, is approximately \result{e008_mde_range} for the participant analysis.
This range is far below the earlier participant-averaged point estimate of \result{e005_apparent}.
The participant analysis would therefore have detected an individual-level effect of that magnitude with at least 80\% probability, although the two estimates belong to different response constructions and estimands.
The participant result does not prove an exactly zero effect.\evd{E005}\evd{E008}

\subsection{Measurability of the 50-component linear target projection}
\label{sec:results-predicted-identification}

Section~\ref{sec:measurement-prerequisites} defines the aligned-target increment \(A(u)\) over nuisance and the correct-pairing increment \(Q(u)\) over the frozen row-twin control, both aggregated across folds so that the participant is the biological inference unit.

The aligned-target increment does not pass the continuation rule carried over from the biological-transfer design.
It is an internal decision rule, and it requires the simultaneous family-95\% one-sided upper bound to exceed \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\).
Let \(\bar{A}\) be the participant mean of \(A(u)\) (\result{e030_participant_n}).
Let \(U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})\) be its simultaneous family-95\% one-sided upper bound and \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\) the continuation threshold.
The result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{A}
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique}},
\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_ci}},
\\
U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_upper}}
<
\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}
=
\text{\result{e030_practical_threshold}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exact-target-measurability-result}
\end{equation}
Only \result{e030_target_positive_n} participant effects are positive, with exact sign test \(p\) = \result{e030_target_sign_p}, and every leave-one-out mean, dropping any single participant or stimulus block, remains negative.
The bound excludes a participant-mean effect of at least \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\) without showing the effect to be zero, and it does so because the point estimate is negative rather than because the estimate is precise. The observed-variance 80\% \ac{mde} for this contrast is \result{e030_target_mde}. \evd{E030}

The participant mean of the correct-pairing increment \(Q(u)\) is \result{e030_target_twin}.
Its two-sided 95\% \(t\) interval is \result{e030_target_twin_ci}.
\result{e030_target_twin_positive_n} participant contrasts are positive, with exact sign test \(p\) = \result{e030_target_twin_sign_p}, but the interval spans zero (Figure~\ref{fig:exact-target-measurability}). \evd{E030}

\subsection{Synthetic-response manipulation checks and attribution status}
\label{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation}

Training changed the saved student.
Across the \result{e016_seed_n} saved technical seeds, a fresh readout recovers more of the synthetic target from the synthetic-response student than from its seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd} student, by \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain} under Equation~\ref{eq:target-uptake-rule}, with two-sided 95\% seed-axis \(t\) interval \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain_ci}.
Every saved technical seed improves recovery, so the saved procedure passes the WikiText target-uptake check and uptake is supported.
These seeds quantify technical stability, not biological or model-population uncertainty. \evd{E016}\evd{E025}

The retained student also moved away from its ordinary \ac{kd} counterpart, by a mean layer-6 centered relative Frobenius distance of \result{e025_tribe_movement_mean}.
Every saved technical seed passes the movement check.
Across the direct saved-arm comparisons, the largest absolute language-quality difference is \result{e025_quality_max_gap} bits per byte, below the frozen \result{e025_quality_margin} margin.
The movement is therefore established at language quality within the declared margin, but not shown to arise from brain-response content. \evd{E025}

On fresh held-out Tuckute sentences, scored beyond the nuisance model, that recovery gain over ordinary \ac{kd} does not reappear.
Absolute target extractability from the synthetic-response students is \result{e030_target_extract}, with two-sided 95\% \(t\) interval \result{e030_target_extract_ci}.
The incremental synthetic-response-minus-ordinary \ac{kd} estimate is instead \result{e030_retention_delta}, with two-sided 95\% \(t\) interval \result{e030_retention_delta_ci}.
Thus, the target is linearly recoverable from the trained students on these sentences, but whether synthetic-response training increased its recoverability beyond ordinary \ac{kd} remains unresolved (Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-manipulation-results}). \evd{E030}

Differences between the synthetic-response and text-derived arms in these manipulation results cannot be attributed to brain-response content, because the text-derived auxiliary-control arm fails \result{e026_check_split} identification checks (Figure~\ref{fig:synthetic-comparator-audit}).
Baseline learnability differs most. Before intervention, ordinary \ac{kd} representations recover mean target \(R^2\) of \result{e026_kd_targets} for the synthetic and text-derived targets, leaving \result{e026_headroom_ratio} times as much remaining room for the synthetic target.
The arm also fails the required target-parity margins for covariance geometry, effective rank, and low-level nuisance predictability.
Training parity is unresolved for initial auxiliary loss and gradient magnitude, which were not retained.

Separately, \result{e026_diagnostic_split} post-training diagnostic margins fall outside the audit's original engineering tolerances, which were descriptive margins and not declared decision rules.
The pre-outcome and baseline mismatches can affect apparent learnability and retained-student movement without any difference in brain-response content. \evd{E026}

\subsection{Saved-student biological-transfer assays}
\label{sec:results-predicted-transfer}

The direct biological-transfer assay compares each synthetic-response student with its seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd} student, averaging folds and technical seeds within each participant so that the participant is the biological inference unit (\result{e025_participant_n}).
The participant-level result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{direct}}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd}},\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd_ci}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:results-direct-biological-transfer}
\end{equation}
Here \(\widehat{\Delta}_{\mathrm{direct}}\) is the participant-mean change in held-out unique \(R^2\) from synthetic-response training relative to ordinary \ac{kd} (Figure~\ref{fig:saved-student-biological-transfer}a).
The participant \(t\) interval spans zero, so participant-general biological transfer is not demonstrated in the tested cohort.
Its scope remains limited to the fixed target, student, layer, nuisance design, sentences, participants, and \ac{roi} endpoint. \evd{E025}

The attribution endpoint, each arm's gain over ordinary \ac{kd}, is numerically positive:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}

&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd}}
-
\left(\mbox{\result{e025_textfeat_kd}}\right)
=
\mbox{\result{e025_participant_delta}},\\

\end{aligned}

\end{equation}
The positive relative contrast arises because the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is more negative than ordinary \ac{kd}, not because the synthetic-response arm has a positive direct effect.
The participant \(t\) interval also spans zero, and \result{e025_participant_positive_n} participant contrasts are positive with exact sign test \(p\) = \result{e025_participant_sign_p}.
The one-sided 95\% participant upper bound \result{e025_participant_ucb} lies below the continuation threshold \result{e030_practical_threshold}, so a participant-mean effect of at least that size is excluded, though not shown to be exactly zero.
The observed-variance 80\% \ac{mde} for this contrast is \result{e025_participant_mde}.
Because the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is not adequately matched (Section~\ref{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation}), this contrast cannot identify an effect of brain-response content. \evd{E025}\evd{E026}

The composed-path diagnostic instead measures whether the synthetic-target component that a fresh readout recovers from the frozen student also predicts recorded responses, through three participant-level estimands (Figure~\ref{fig:saved-student-biological-transfer}b):

&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs}},
&
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\widehat{O})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs_ci}},
\\
\widehat{D}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta}},
&
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\widehat{D})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta_ci}},
\\
\widehat{J}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin}},
&
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}(\widehat{J})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin_ci}}.

Here, \(O\) measures absolute aligned predictive overlap, \(D\) measures its increment over seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd}, and \(J\) asks whether that increment is larger for correctly aligned targets than for the frozen row twin.

These quantities are predictive-path diagnostics, not causal-mediation estimates. \evd{E030}

\subsection{Practical-utility evaluation}
\label{sec:results-utility}

The only completed practical-utility evaluation compares the averaged recorded-response arm with its approximately quality-matched permuted-response control across \result{e009_seed_n} technical training seeds.
Negative values of that contrast (Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-ratio}) favor the recorded-response arm.
Averaging that contrast across seeds gives
\begin{equation}
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{Pereira}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_pereira_recorded_permuted}},
\qquad
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{LeBel}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_lebel_recorded_permuted}}.
\label{eq:results-practical-utility}
\end{equation}
Both mean contrasts, for Pereira sentences~\autocite{pereira2018} and LeBel story text~\autocite{lebel2023}, favor the recorded-response arm (Figure~\ref{fig:bounded-practical-utility}a).
The completed analysis retained no paired technical-seed uncertainty interval or formal equivalence test.
The endpoint contrasts are therefore unresolved. \evd{E009}

The endpoint means can be credited to brain-response training only if that training reliably changed controlled brain predictivity, the prerequisite contrast \(A_s\) of Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-prerequisite}.
Its seed-level summary is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{mean}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mean}},
\qquad
\operatorname{median}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_median}},
\qquad
\mathrm{MDE}_{80\%}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mde}}.
\label{eq:results-utility-prerequisite}
\end{equation}
The \ac{mde} is a sensitivity reference, not an acceptance threshold or equivalence margin.

Only \result{e009_positive_seed_signs} of the technical-seed prerequisite contrasts are positive.
Seed~0 contributes \result{e009_seed0_gap}, whereas the remaining seeds average approximately \result{e009_other_seven_mean}.
The mixed signs and seed-0 influence leave the controlled-predictivity prerequisite not demonstrated across technical seeds (Figure~\ref{fig:bounded-practical-utility}b). \evd{E009}
