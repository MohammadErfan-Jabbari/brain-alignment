\subsection{Controlled brain predictivity under regional and naturalistic assays}
\label{sec:results-signal}

Trained representations exceeded the declared nuisance baseline in the controlled regional predictivity assay.
For the primary Qwen2.5-0.5B layer-12 comparison, controlled unique \(R^2\) was \result{e002_qwen_unique}, where the \(\pm\) term is the descriptive standard deviation across five contiguous stimulus folds.
The recorded trained-minus-untrained difference is \result{e002_qwen_gap}, but the executed runner built the static nuisance block separately for each model.
That gap therefore does not cleanly isolate learned parameters against one fixed nuisance design.
Because we averaged the recorded responses across five participants before readout fitting, the folds describe stimulus-block variation and do not support participant-level inference.
The assay therefore supports controlled regional predictivity for this fixed participant-averaged target.\evd{E002}

Trained representations also exceeded their matched untrained controls in the controlled naturalistic voxelwise assay when complete stories were held out.
For participant UTS03, the mean differences were \result{e006_gpt2_gap} for GPT-2 Small and \result{e006_qwen_gap} for Qwen2.5-0.5B, and all five held-out-story blocks had positive differences.
The voxels are spatially dependent, and the story folds share training stories, so neither the voxel set nor the story folds provide independent biological replication.
This assay therefore supports a within-participant controlled brain predictivity result conditional on UTS03 and the fixed naturalistic~protocol.\evd{E006}

Together, the assays show that the tested trained \ac{lm} representations contain linearly accessible information useful for predicting held-out recorded \ac{fmri} responses beyond their model-specific nuisance features (Figure~\ref{fig:controlled-predictivity-results}); learned-parameter attribution from the regional gap remains unresolved.

The assays do not identify a shared computation between the \acp{lm} and the brain or establish population-general controlled brain predictivity.
They also do not test whether using brain responses during training changes a retained student or improves language behavior.

\subsection{Observed distillation headroom and its association with language quality}
\label{sec:results-headroom}

The from-scratch GPT-2 comparison contains an observed teacher-relative gap, but it does not establish quality-aware headroom (a teacher--student gap measured at comparable language quality) for the intervention routes.
Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-retention} places the GPT-2 teacher, floor, and student conditions on the floor-anchored retention scale defined in Equation~\ref{eq:floor-anchored-retention}.
At the fixed Tuckute layers, the randomly initialized GPT-2 Small logit-\ac{kd} student is \result{e003_kd_cold_delta} below the GPT-2 Medium teacher in controlled brain predictivity (bootstrap no-drop \(p = \result{e003_kd_cold_nodrop_p}\) over the cold arm's folds and seeds).
Relative to the architecture-matched untrained GPT-2 Small floor, the student retains \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_point} of the teacher-to-floor interval.
The recorded 95\% bootstrap interval over folds and seeds is \result{e003_kd_cold_retention_ci}.\evd{E003}
The tested cold-start route therefore leaves an observed gap within this assay, although the comparison does not identify its source.

Language quality is a material competing explanation.
The randomly initialized student remains substantially weaker as an \ac{lm}, with perplexity \result{e003_kd_cold_ppl}, compared with \result{e003_teacher_ppl} for the teacher.\evd{E003}
The evaluated conditions also differ in initialization and training history, not only in objective, so the ordered predictivity estimates do not show that compression or \ac{kd} caused the gap.
The pretrained GPT-2 Small condition is closer to the teacher in initialization and language quality, which makes it the relevant reference for the synthetic-response route.
The completed analysis did not establish a formal quality-aware headroom result for that warm route.
The recorded-response intervention uses Qwen2.5-1.5B and Qwen2.5-0.5B\evd{E009}, for which we did not measure route-matched teacher--student headroom.\evd{E003}\evd{E015}

The broader model comparison shows why later interventions must control language quality.
Across \result{e015_full_n} decoder-only \acp{lm} from six families, participant-averaged controlled brain predictivity is negatively associated with log bits per byte, where higher values indicate poorer language quality.\evd{E015}
Pearson \(r\) is \result{e015_full_corr}, with a family-cluster 95\% \ac{ci} of \result{e015_full_ci}.\evd{E015}
Figure~\ref{fig:distillation-headroom-quality} shows this full-range association and marks the capable-model band, defined as the \result{e015_capable_n} models with bits per byte below \result{e015_capable_bpb_max}.
Within that band, Pearson \(r\) is \result{e015_capable_corr}, with a Fisher 95\% \ac{ci} of \result{e015_capable_ci}.\evd{E015}
The point estimate remains moderately negative, but its interval includes zero.
Model family, scale, training data, and language quality remain entangled, so the association identifies language quality as a design constraint rather than a causal explanation.

Together, the results support an observed, assay-conditional gap between the GPT-2 teacher and randomly initialized student.
Quality-aware headroom remains unresolved for the warm GPT-2 route and unmeasured for the Qwen route.
They do not establish that the headroom is brain-specific or recoverable through a brain-response objective.
A valid intervention comparison must therefore test the brain-response objective against a same-procedure control while maintaining comparable \ac{lm} quality.

\subsection{Recorded-response intervention at valid inference units}
\label{sec:results-recorded}

Participant-averaged recorded responses produce positive recorded-response-minus-permuted-response point estimates at the shared-fold inference unit.
In the loss-form screen that selected the auxiliary response loss, the mean contrast is \result{e004_qwen_specific}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e004_qwen_fold_ci}.\evd{E004}
In the \ac{kd}-anchored paired intervention, the mean contrast is \result{e005_apparent}, with a five-fold \(t\) interval of \result{e005_fold_ci}.\evd{E005}
The recorded-response arm and permuted-response control have perplexities \result{e005_real_ppl} and \result{e005_permuted_ppl}, respectively, showing no gross quality imbalance without establishing a formal equivalence margin.\evd{E005}
Both intervals include zero, and both estimates are sensitive to a dominant fold.
The point estimates are positive, but a pairing advantage is not demonstrated.

These averaged-response comparisons answer a narrower question than participant-level inference.
Averaging responses before training creates one shared stimulus-locked target, so the fold-level contrast asks whether correct stimulus--response pairing helps for that fixed target.
It does not estimate the mean intervention effect across participants.

The participant-count diagnostic probes how the contrast varies with the number of participants.
In the original nested sequence each subset contained the previous one, so participant count varied together with participant identity; with random subsets instead, the diagnostic shows no stable monotone relationship.\evd{E010}
With the model representations held fixed, averaging nevertheless increases controlled brain predictivity by reducing response noise and emphasizing the shared response component; individual responses still contain measurable signal.\evd{E014}
Averaging can therefore strengthen measurement of the shared response without creating biological replication.

The participant-specific intervention does not demonstrate a participant-general advantage at the biological inference unit, the participant.
After the technical seed and fold repetitions are aggregated within each participant as defined in Section~\ref{sec:recorded-intervention}, the mean recorded-response-minus-permuted-response effect across nine participants is \result{e008_mean}, with participant \(t\) interval \result{e008_ci}.\evd{E008}
The shared-fold summary is also centered near zero at \result{e008_fold_mean}, with five-fold \(t\) interval \result{e008_fold_ci}.\evd{E008}

The minimum detectable effect at 80\% power, computed from the observed variance, is approximately \result{e008_mde_range} for the participant analysis.
This range is far below the earlier participant-averaged point estimate of \result{e005_apparent}.
The participant analysis would therefore have detected an individual-level effect of that magnitude on this scale with at least 80\% probability, although the averaged-target estimate belongs to a different response construction and estimand.
The participant result neither proves an exactly zero effect nor extends beyond the tested target, model, cohort, objective, and readout.\evd{E005}\evd{E008}

\subsection{Measurability of the 50-component linear target projection}
\label{sec:results-predicted-identification}

Section~\ref{sec:measurement-prerequisites} defines \(A(u)\) as the aligned-target increment over nuisance for participant \(u\), and \(Q(u)\) as the added value of correct sentence--target pairing over the frozen row-twin control.
Both are aggregated across folds within each participant, so the participant is the biological inference unit.

The aligned-target increment does not pass the continuation rule carried over from the biological-transfer design.
Let \(\bar{A}\) be the participant mean of \(A(u)\) (\result{e030_participant_n}).
Let \(U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})\) be the upper endpoint of its two-sided Bonferroni family-95\% interval and \(\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}\) the continuation threshold.
The result is
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\bar{A}
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique}},
\\
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_ci}},
\\
U^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\bar{A})
&=
\text{\result{e030_target_unique_upper}}
<
\tau_{\mathrm{cont}}
=
\text{\result{e030_practical_threshold}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:exact-target-measurability-result}
\end{equation}

The frozen row twin preserves the target rows and readout procedure while breaking their pairing with sentences.

\subsection{Synthetic-response manipulation checks and attribution status}
\label{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation}

Training changed the saved student even though the separate target-projection assay did not meet its continuation rule.
The first manipulation check asks whether a fresh readout recovers more of the synthetic target from the synthetic-response student than from its seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd} student.
Across the \result{e016_seed_n} saved technical seeds, the mean recovery difference of Equation~\ref{eq:target-uptake-rule} is \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain}, with two-sided 95\% seed-axis \(t\) interval \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain_ci}.
Every saved technical seed improves recovery, so the saved procedure passes the WikiText target-uptake check.
These seeds quantify technical stability, not biological or model-population uncertainty. \evd{E016}\evd{E025}

The retained student also moved while measured language quality remained within the declared margin.
Mean movement from the ordinary \ac{kd} student, measured by layer-6 centered relative Frobenius distance, is \result{e025_tribe_movement_mean}.
Every saved technical seed passes the movement check.
Across the direct saved-arm comparisons, the largest absolute language-quality difference is \result{e025_quality_max_gap} bits per byte, below the frozen \result{e025_quality_margin} margin.
These results show that the intervention changed the retained student without a detected language-quality discrepancy larger than the declared margin.
They do not show that brain-response content caused the change or improved language quality. \evd{E025}

On held-out Tuckute sentences, scored beyond the nuisance model, the same contrast does not reproduce that WikiText recovery gain.
Absolute target extractability from the synthetic-response students is \result{e030_target_extract}, with Bonferroni family-95\% interval \result{e030_target_extract_ci}.
The incremental synthetic-response-minus-ordinary \ac{kd} estimate is instead \result{e030_retention_delta}, with Bonferroni family-95\% interval \result{e030_retention_delta_ci}.
Thus, the target is linearly recoverable from the trained students on these sentences, but whether synthetic-response training increased its recoverability beyond ordinary \ac{kd} remains unresolved. \evd{E030}

Differences between the arms in these manipulation results cannot be attributed to brain-response content, because the available text-derived auxiliary-control arm is not adequately matched.
The text-derived auxiliary-control arm fails \result{e026_check_split} identification checks.
Baseline learnability differs most: before intervention, ordinary \ac{kd} representations recover mean target \(R^2\) of \result{e026_kd_targets} for the synthetic and text-derived targets, respectively.
This leaves \result{e026_headroom_ratio} times as much remaining room for target recovery for the synthetic target.
The arm also fails the required target-parity margins for covariance geometry, effective rank, and low-level nuisance predictability.
Training parity is unresolved for initial auxiliary loss and gradient magnitude, which were not retained.

Separately, \result{e026_diagnostic_split} post-training diagnostic margins depart from their original engineering ranges.
The pre-outcome and baseline mismatches can affect apparent learnability and retained-student movement without any difference in brain-response content.
The audit does not establish which mismatch caused the observed cross-target difference. \evd{E026}

\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}

&=
\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd}},\\

\mbox{\result{e025_tribe_kd_ci}}.

\left(\mbox{\result{e025_textfeat_kd}}\right)
=
\mbox{\result{e025_participant_delta}},\\
\mathrm{CI}_{95\%}
&=
\mbox{\result{e025_participant_ci}}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:results-control-transfer-decomposition}
\end{equation}
The positive relative contrast arises because the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is more negative than ordinary \ac{kd}, not because the synthetic-response arm has a positive direct effect.
The participant \(t\) interval also spans zero.
The one-sided 95\% participant upper bound \result{e025_participant_ucb} lies below the continuation threshold \result{e030_practical_threshold}, so a participant-mean effect of at least that size is excluded, though not shown to be exactly zero.
The observed-variance 80\% \ac{mde} for this contrast is \result{e025_participant_mde}.
Section~\ref{sec:results-synthetic-manipulation} shows that the text-derived auxiliary-control arm is not adequately matched as a comparator.
The contrast therefore cannot identify an effect of brain-response content. \evd{E025}\evd{E026}

The composed-path diagnostic asks a different question.
It measures whether the synthetic-target component that a fresh readout recovers from the frozen student also predicts recorded responses.
Figure~\ref{fig:saved-student-biological-transfer}b presents its three participant-level estimands:
\begin{align}
\widehat{O}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{O})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_abs_ci}},
\\
\widehat{D}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{D})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_delta_ci}},
\\
\widehat{J}
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin}},
&
\mathrm{CI}^{\mathrm{family}}_{95\%}(\widehat{J})
&=
\mbox{\result{e030_overlap_twin_ci}}.
\end{align}
Here, \(O\) measures absolute aligned predictive overlap, \(D\) measures its increment over seed-matched ordinary \ac{kd}, and \(J\) asks whether that increment is larger for correctly aligned targets than for the frozen row twin.
Every Bonferroni family-95\% interval spans zero.
The composed-path diagnostic therefore leaves predictive overlap, incremental overlap over ordinary \ac{kd}, and any aligned-over-row-twin increment unresolved under participant inference.
These quantities are predictive-path diagnostics, not causal-mediation estimates. \evd{E030}

\subsection{Practical-utility evaluation}
\label{sec:results-utility}

The only completed practical-utility evaluation compares the averaged recorded-response arm with its approximately quality-matched permuted-response control across \result{e009_seed_n} technical training seeds.
Negative values of the recorded-response-minus-permuted-response contrast defined in Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-ratio} favor the recorded-response arm.
Averaging that contrast across seeds gives
\begin{equation}
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{Pereira}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_pereira_recorded_permuted}},
\qquad
\overline{\Delta R}_{\mathrm{LeBel}}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_lebel_recorded_permuted}}.
\label{eq:results-practical-utility}
\end{equation}
These are the mean contrasts for Pereira sentences~\autocite{pereira2018} and LeBel story text~\autocite{lebel2023}, respectively.
Both point estimates favor the recorded-response arm.
The completed analysis retained no paired technical-seed uncertainty interval or formal equivalence test, and an \ac{mde} cannot substitute for either.
The endpoint contrasts are therefore unresolved. \evd{E009}

The endpoint means can be credited to brain-response training only if that training reliably changed controlled brain predictivity.
Equation~\ref{eq:practical-utility-prerequisite} defines this prerequisite contrast in controlled brain predictivity as \(A_s\).
Its seed-level summary is
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{mean}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mean}},
\qquad
\operatorname{median}_s(A_s)
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_median}},
\qquad
\mathrm{MDE}_{80\%}
=
\mbox{\result{e009_fulcrum_mde}}.
\label{eq:results-utility-prerequisite}
\end{equation}
The \ac{mde} in Equation~\ref{eq:results-utility-prerequisite} is a sensitivity reference for the prerequisite contrast, not an acceptance threshold or equivalence margin.

Only \result{e009_positive_seed_signs} of the technical-seed prerequisite contrasts are positive.
Seed~0 contributes \result{e009_seed0_gap}, whereas the remaining seeds average approximately \result{e009_other_seven_mean}.
The mixed signs and seed-0 influence leave the controlled-predictivity prerequisite not demonstrated across technical seeds. \evd{E009}
