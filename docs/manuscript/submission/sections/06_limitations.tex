\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

Section~\ref{sec:discussion} localized where support stopped in the recorded-response and synthetic-response routes.
The reach of those conclusions is bounded by population and measurement coverage, identification and inference, and the intervention and utility space searched.

\subsection{Population, data, and measurement scope}

The biological evidence is limited to \ac{fmri} responses to English-language stimuli from the studied cohorts.
The sentence-level assays summarize responses within fixed left-hemisphere language \acp{roi}, while the naturalistic voxelwise predictivity assay uses reliability-selected voxels from one deeply sampled story listener.
Some recorded-response targets average responses across participants to emphasize a shared stimulus-locked component; the participant-level analyses instead retain one aggregated effect per person.
The conclusions therefore concern these isolated-sentence and spoken-story response summaries, not language processing across the whole brain or population at large.\evd{E002}\evd{E006}\evd{E008}\evd{E025}

The study does not cover other brain-recording modalities, including those with finer temporal resolution, populations that differ in language background or clinical and developmental status, non-English or nonlinguistic stimuli, or broader reading, listening, and interactive contexts.
It also does not test whether information unavailable to the declared linear ridge readouts is accessible through nonlinear mappings or at different temporal scales.
These untested settings may change what brain information is measured and how that information can enter training.
The route-specific conclusions therefore do not extend to these settings.\evd{E006}\evd{E030}

\subsection{Identification and inference limitations}

What each result identifies depends on the nuisance set, comparator, readout, and inference unit.
Unique predictivity excludes only the included nuisance features, while the trained-versus-untrained contrast establishes learned predictive access rather than a shared biological or computational mechanism.\evd{E002}\evd{E006}
The text-derived auxiliary-control arm differs from the synthetic target in baseline predictability, remaining headroom, covariance geometry, effective rank, and nuisance predictability, so its cross-target contrast cannot identify the contribution of brain-response content.\evd{E026}
The 50-component linear target-projection measurability result is restricted to the declared \ac{pca} assay, and specificity against the frozen row-twin control remains unresolved.\evd{E030}
Folds, stories, voxels, and training seeds are dependent technical measurements that must be aggregated within each participant before population inference.\evd{E008}\evd{E025}
Even after this aggregation, the fixed or shared stimulus sets do not support generalization to all language stimuli.
Appendix~\ref{app:inference} formalizes these identification and dependence boundaries.

The study contains positive checks for separate claim roles: the measurement assays support controlled brain predictivity, and the synthetic-response intervention satisfies the target-uptake and retained-student-movement criteria.
It does not contain a known-positive brain-response intervention that is expected to improve participant-level biological transfer through the complete evaluation path.
The positive component checks therefore show that the readouts detect existing signal and that training changes the retained student; they do not establish the sensitivity of the full intervention-to-transfer path to a genuine brain-specific benefit.\evd{E002}\evd{E006}\evd{E025}
This limitation leaves open whether a smaller or differently structured benefit would pass the complete path, subject to the interval and minimum-detectable-effect bounds reported in Section~\ref{sec:results-predicted-transfer}.

Within those design bounds, the participant-level and exact-target intervals reported in Sections~\ref{sec:results-recorded}, \ref{sec:results-predicted-identification}, and \ref{sec:results-predicted-transfer} disfavor the tested pairing, transfer, and target-measurability contrasts at the relevant internal continuation scales.
They do not establish that the corresponding effects are exactly zero.
The direct synthetic-response versus ordinary-\ac{kd} transfer contrast is interpretable independently of the auxiliary comparator.
Only brain-response-content attribution is blocked by the non-identifying comparator, while target-row specificity in the 50-component linear assay remains unresolved.
Smaller average contrasts across participants, heterogeneous participant effects, and effects in other cohorts remain compatible with the evidence.\evd{E008}\evd{E025}\evd{E026}\evd{E030}

\subsection{Intervention and utility scope}

The intervention search was diagnostic rather than exhaustive.
The central synthetic-response route tested a GPT-2 Small student paired with a GPT-2 Medium teacher, representing one teacher--student scale pairing within one architecture family.\evd{E016}
Across both routes, the study did not jointly explore broader student architectures, objective families, loss weights, schedules, data volumes, target constructions, or participant-personalization strategies.
The executed adapter-capacity, contrastive, voxelwise, and full-parameter variants inform only their tested parameterizations and data regimes.\evd{E011}\evd{E013}\evd{E017}
Section~\ref{sec:results-recorded} and Appendix~\ref{app:secondary} report the individual variant designs and dispositions.
Different model scales, targets, objectives, architectures, and optimization procedures remain open.

Section~\ref{sec:results-utility} evaluates practical utility through a narrow set of endpoints.
Perplexity served both as a language-quality control and as the primary out-of-domain utility measure, so the study does not exhaust task accuracy, calibration, robustness, sample efficiency, or deployment-specific value.
The completed endpoint analysis reports descriptive means across eight technical seeds but no paired endpoint interval or equivalence test, so the direct endpoint advantage remains unresolved.
The recorded-response route also did not establish a technically stable change in controlled brain predictivity required to interpret that endpoint contrast as brain-specific utility.\evd{E009}
No synthetic-response utility test was run.
Sections~\ref{sec:results-predicted-identification} and \ref{sec:results-predicted-transfer} report that the 50-component linear target-projection assay did not meet its internal continuation criterion and that participant-level biological transfer was not demonstrated.\evd{E025}\evd{E030}
The supporting studies summarized in Appendix~\ref{app:secondary} tested narrow design levers, stopped before intervention, or stopped because the planned measurement was unusable.\evd{E019}\evd{E020}\evd{E022}\evd{E024}
They therefore do not broaden the set of completed endpoints that identify brain-specific utility.
No completed route supports a brain-specific practical-utility claim under the tested procedures.
Practical effects at untested endpoints after a successfully identified biological intervention remain open.
