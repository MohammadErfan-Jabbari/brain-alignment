\begin{abstract}
Brain alignment measures whether a model representation can predict recorded human brain responses to the same language stimuli on held-out examples.
Successful prediction establishes linearly accessible brain-relevant information under a specified readout and control set, but it does not establish a shared biological mechanism.
This thesis asks whether recorded or predicted neural responses can also provide useful auxiliary supervision when training a smaller language model by knowledge distillation.
The experiments optimize neural-target losses rather than the encoding score itself, then evaluate the retained students with held-out, nuisance-controlled brain encoding.
Trained language-model features contain measurable signal beyond the specified nuisances, but the tested objectives based directly on recorded fMRI do not establish a participant-level benefit at comparable language-model quality.
With a fresh ridge readout fitted after training, TRIBE-supervised students achieved held-out synthetic-target $R^2$ \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain} higher than seed-matched KD students \evd{E016}\evd{E026}.
Their retained parameters and representations also changed while language quality remained within the predeclared envelope \evd{E025}\evd{E026}.
The saved text-feature target was not a valid control for attributing that difference to neural target content.
TRIBE's KD baseline left \result{e026_headroom_ratio} times as much remaining headroom as the text-feature KD baseline.
The targets differed in covariance geometry and nuisance predictability, and the trained arms differed in parameter displacement and centered-Frobenius representation change \evd{E026}.
Across nine participants, the mean direct contrast in held-out unique $R^2$, TRIBE minus KD, was \result{e025_tribe_kd}, with a 95\% CI of \result{e025_tribe_kd_ci} \evd{E025}.
The predeclared primary mean contrast, TRIBE minus textfeat, was \result{e025_participant_delta} unique $R^2$, with a 95\% CI of \result{e025_participant_ci}.
Its one-sided 95\% upper confidence bound was \result{e025_participant_ucb}, below the internal $+0.002$ continuation threshold \evd{E025}.
Because the comparator was not valid for target-content attribution, this relative contrast cannot isolate an effect of neural target content \evd{E026}.
On the same 1,000 sentences, the PCA-50 text-only TRIBE target's participant-mean unique linear increment above nuisance was \result{e030_target_unique}, with a Bonferroni family-95\% two-sided interval of \result{e030_target_unique_ci}; the upper endpoint is below the inherited $+0.002$ reference, while the aligned-minus-frozen-twin contrast remains unresolved \evd{E030}.
These results separate exact-substrate target measurability, target uptake, retained-model movement, comparator identification, and participant-level biological transfer, and they do not demonstrate a reliable brain-guided training benefit in the tested distillation regimes.
\end{abstract}
