A reliable linear map predicts human language-network activity from a language model's middle-layer activations, and this is almost always used to \emph{measure} alignment after training.
This thesis asks whether that alignment can instead be \emph{used} as a training signal inside model compression, in the one cell the 2026 literature leaves empty: distilling a smaller student while brain alignment is in the objective, at a matched budget.
An information budget argument predicts that a brain signal can act only as a weak prior, with whatever value it has concentrated in low-data and aggressively compressed regimes rather than acting as a second teacher.
The program is a sequence of kill-gated questions, and the first and foundational one is answered here: a trained model's middle layer carries unique, out-of-sample variance about real language-network activity that a same-architecture untrained network does not, with a trained-minus-untrained gap of \result{e006_gpt2_gap} for GPT-2 and \result{e006_qwen_gap} for Qwen2.5-0.5B at voxel scale, positive on 95 to 99\% of reliable voxels after a capacity-fair low-level nuisance regression under contiguous splits \evd{E006}.
The next question is answered too: plain perplexity-only distillation does not preserve that alignment by default, a from-scratch student trained on the teacher's outputs alone falling well below the teacher along a monotone gradient \evd{E003}.
Whether the loss is specific to the distillation objective or simply tracks the language-model quality the student gives up is left open at this screen.

\end{abstract}
