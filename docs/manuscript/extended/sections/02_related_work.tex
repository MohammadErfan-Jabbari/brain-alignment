The work on language models and the brain organizes on two axes, and on those axes three of the four cells are occupied while the fourth, where this work sits, is empty.
The first axis is what an intervention does to the parameter budget: it either \emph{grows} the model (fine-tunes, adds adapters, attaches a brain-prediction head) or \emph{shrinks} it (quantizes, prunes, or distills into a smaller student).
The second is how it treats brain alignment: either as a quantity \emph{measured} after the fact, or as one \emph{optimized} inside the training objective.
Three of the four cells are occupied; the fourth is empty, and it is the thesis (Table~\ref{tab:cells}).

\subsection{Grown models, alignment measured: the classical brain-score literature}

The largest body of work fits encoding models to frozen, pretrained networks and reads off how alignment varies with architecture, scale, and training.
Alignment is carried by structured middle-layer representations, with syntactic structure driving the layerwise trend and semantics more region-specific \autocite{oota2023}.
It rises with model scale and saturates in the few-billion-parameter range; at matched parameter count, scale rather than instruction-tuning moves it, which is why a base model is the appropriate alignment teacher \autocite{gao2024}.
Past that range the relationship can decouple and even reverse, as the largest models outgrow the human language network and track formal linguistic competence more than behavior \autocite{alkhamissi2025}.
This cell establishes that the signal exists and is non-trivial, but it never intervenes through alignment, so it cannot speak to whether alignment is usable.

\subsection{Grown models, alignment optimized: brain-tuning, and why the bare inversion is not novel}

Several studies put alignment in the objective of a fine-tuned or grown model, across modalities.
For speech, a voxelwise loss fine-tunes compact encoders and improves both alignment and several downstream tasks, with a multi-participant variant reporting further gains at higher fMRI-data efficiency \autocite{moussa2025,moussa2025b}.
For text, fMRI-augmented training of GPT-2 and LLaMA-2 with a cosine-similarity brain loss beats text-only baselines, though the reported gains are mixed across models and the splits are random rather than contiguous with no shuffled-brain control, so they would not pass the anti-confound bar adopted here \autocite{bilgin2026}.
Causally, driving text models \emph{away} from alignment at matched perplexity damages performance across a 200-plus-task suite, and the same study's positive arm shows that driving toward alignment helps, evidence that the recoverable capability is real \autocite{merlin2026}.
The bare inverted idea is therefore not novel; what no study in this cell does is compress.
Every one of them grows or holds the parameter budget, which leaves the compression question entirely open.

\subsection{Shrunk models, alignment measured: the compression study that does not close the gap}

One study compresses language models and tracks alignment, and it is the closest prior work to this thesis, so its scope needs a precise reading.
It finds that alignment survives only particular post-training quantizers on models of at least a few billion parameters; other quantizers, smaller models, and pruning all degrade it, and it never tests knowledge distillation \autocite{oota2026}.
Critically, this is \emph{shrink} crossed with \emph{measure}, not with \emph{optimize}: it observes alignment under a fixed-function perturbation of the weights and never makes it a training target.
Distillation is a different and lossier operation: it re-fits a smaller function from scratch against the teacher, the regime most likely to discard the middle-layer structure where alignment lives, so a result about quantization does not transfer to it.
The data-processing inequality frames why the question is quantitative: a student can only lose alignment relative to its teacher, never create it, and in the narrow regime where preservation holds (particular quantizers on models of at least a few billion parameters) that bound is nearly tight; the open question is whether distillation, a lossier transform, sits well below tightness by default and whether a brain objective pulls the student back toward the bound.

\subsection{The empty cell, and the adjacent precedents that motivate it weakly}

The remaining cell, shrink the model while alignment is optimized at a matched student budget, is unoccupied, and it is where this work sits.
Two precedents from vision and audio are sometimes read as support for a brain regularizer in the low-data regime, but each weakens under scrutiny.
A neural-data regularizer on a vision network improves accuracy and adversarial robustness, but a shuffled-label control reproduces the accuracy gain, so only the robustness benefit there requires real neural structure \autocite{pirlot2022}.
fMRI fine-tuning of a compact audio network improves a majority of downstream tasks, but the apparent low-data advantage carries no interaction test and no matched non-brain control, so it is suggestive rather than isolated \autocite{freteault2025}.
These precedents are methodological cautions more than positive evidence: isolating the brain-specific increment requires the matched non-brain control both studies omitted, and neither supplies a result that transfers cleanly to the distillation regime.

\subsection{The measurement-validity bar, and the fMRI-free surrogate question}

A parallel line argues that brain-alignment scores are easy to inflate and easy to over-read, and it sets the standard this work measures against.
Shuffled splits leak through temporal autocorrelation, low-level and static features carry much of the apparent signal, and audited frozen-encoding scores can fail basic predictive and reliability gates \autocite{hadidi2026,jia2026}.
A related caution is that aggregate scores hide mechanism: the same headline number can be genuine semantics in one model and shallow phonology in another \autocite{oota2024}.
A more ambitious route would replace the live fMRI loss with a differentiable surrogate, but the literature does not yet agree on which way to push one: local intrinsic dimension correlates with alignment in opposite directions across language and vision studies, and a similarity-based surrogate is confounded by width and depth, with only neighborhood-overlap surviving null calibration \autocite{cheng2026,yu2026,groger2026}.
A surrogate objective is therefore held as future work, since its very direction must first be validated against real fMRI.
