\section{Conclusion}

This thesis asked whether nuisance-controlled linear brain alignment can do more than describe a trained language model: can it guide the training of a smaller student?
The measurement premise holds for trained language-model features in the tested settings.
Trained middle layers predict held-out fMRI beyond the recorded nuisance variables and untrained-network controls \evd{E002}\evd{E006}.
Plain distillation also leaves measurable alignment headroom, although the amount attributable to distillation rather than language-model quality remains unresolved \evd{E003}\evd{E015}.

The intervention premise was not supported in the tested regimes.
Recorded-target objectives do not establish a reliable participant-level gain, and changing adapter capacity, loss form, target resolution, or trainable parameter count does not rescue the result \evd{E008}\evd{E011}\evd{E013}\evd{E017}.
The dense-proxy experiment passes target-uptake, student-movement, and language-quality gates, but its saved cross-target comparator fails measured-axis identification; on the exact endpoint substrate, the PCA-50 target also does not pass the frozen $+0.002$ participant-mean unique-linear-measurability rule, while its advantage over the frozen twin remains unresolved \evd{E016}\evd{E025}\evd{E026}\evd{E030}.
On recorded fMRI, direct participant-level TRIBE-minus-KD is \result{e025_tribe_kd}, with 95\% CI \result{e025_tribe_kd_ci}.
The preregistered relative comparison has a one-sided 95\% upper bound of \result{e025_participant_ucb}, below the internal $+0.002$ continuation threshold, but E026 prevents that comparison from identifying neural target content \evd{E025}\evd{E026}.

These results are limited to the models, objectives, participants, stimuli, and endpoints studied here.
They do not show that neural supervision is universally ineffective.
They show that a positive encoding score, successful proxy fit, or repeated optimization effect is not enough to establish a useful brain-guided intervention.

Future claims should separate controlled measurement, exact-substrate target validity, target uptake, comparator assessment, biological transfer, and downstream utility, and define the estimand for each quantitative stage.
Language quality must be controlled, technical repetitions must not replace biological units, and synthetic or averaged targets must transfer to independently recorded responses.
Under those requirements, trained-model brain alignment remains a measurable readout under the stated controls in this study, but not a demonstrated reliable training signal for the tested compression regimes.
