\section{Discussion}

\subsection{Main empirical answer}

The study gives different answers to measurement and intervention.
Under the recorded controls, trained middle-layer representations predict held-out brain responses beyond the nuisance set and same-architecture untrained networks \evd{E002}\evd{E006}.
Plain distillation leaves a student below its teacher on the alignment measure, but that gap remains confounded with language-model quality \evd{E003}\evd{E015}.
These findings establish a usable readout and measurable headroom, not a training benefit.

The dense-proxy branch separates five distinctions in one saved-student design: exact-substrate target measurability, target uptake, student movement, comparator validity, and transfer to recorded fMRI.
TRIBE-target prediction improves over KD by \result{e016_tribe_synth_gain}, the retained students move, and language quality remains within the declared tolerance \evd{E016}\evd{E025}.
The saved text-feature comparator then fails \result{e026_check_split} frozen checks, so the cross-target proxy advantage cannot identify neural target content \evd{E026}.
On recorded fMRI, direct participant-level TRIBE-minus-KD is \result{e025_tribe_kd}, with 95\% CI \result{e025_tribe_kd_ci}.
The preregistered relative estimate has a one-sided 95\% upper bound of \result{e025_participant_ucb}, below the internal $+0.002$ continuation threshold, but that relative estimate is not content-identifying after E026 \evd{E025}\evd{E026}.

The recorded-target experiments reach the same practical boundary by other routes.
The tested MSE, contrastive, higher-capacity, voxelwise, and full-fine-tuning variants do not establish a participant-level benefit, and the downstream experiments do not establish a practical payoff \evd{E004}\evd{E008}\evd{E009}\evd{E011}\evd{E013}\evd{E017}.
In these models and datasets, trained middle-layer brain alignment is measurable, but the tested alignment objectives do not provide a reliable training signal for the studied compression regimes.
This does not imply that neural supervision is ineffective in every model, modality, dataset, or optimization setting.

The experiments also show why target fit alone is insufficient.
An auxiliary target can become easier to predict because it offers more headroom or a different covariance geometry.
Repeated success across seeds measures optimization repeatability, not biological generalization.
Comparable language quality removes one alternative explanation but does not identify the content of the changed representation.
A training claim needs an appropriate target control, inference at the biological unit named by the claim, and transfer to a recorded endpoint that was not optimized during training.

\subsection{Where the evidence chain breaks}

For objectives trained directly on recorded responses, the first break is between controlled measurement and a reliable intervention effect.
A linear readout can predict recorded responses from a frozen representation even when the tested auxiliary objectives fail to produce a stable held-out gain.
The results are consistent with competition from the language objective, low-sample noisy targets, and language-quality damage under stronger updates, but they do not identify which mechanism caused the failure.
The fold-level E004 result, the participant-level E008 result, and the neutral-to-harmful voxelwise trajectory in E013 each locate a failure at this intervention stage under a different readout or inference unit \evd{E004} \evd{E008} \evd{E013}.

Increasing flexibility does not by itself resolve the problem.
Higher-rank LoRA changes capacity without producing a stronger effective manipulation, a contrastive ROI objective does not rescue the participant-level effect, and full fine-tuning ranges from substantial language-quality damage to a near-zero real-minus-control contrast \evd{E011}\evd{E013}\evd{E017}.
These results implicate the tested induction mechanisms rather than one adapter rank or one loss parameterization.
They do not rule out objectives with different targets, architectures, temporal models, or optimization regimes.

For the dense proxy, E030 identifies an earlier scoped break: the exact PCA-50 target does not pass the participant-mean unique-linear-measurability rule on the endpoint substrate \evd{E030}.
E016 and E025 still establish target uptake, student movement, comparable language quality, and a valid net TRIBE-versus-KD endpoint, but they do not show that a measurably valid target signal was lost during transport.
E026 separately prevents the cross-target gain from identifying brain-derived content \evd{E016}\evd{E025}\evd{E026}.

\subsection{Inference unit and response averaging}

The inference unit changes the scientific answer because different repetitions support different claims.
Pooling three seeds over five shared stimulus folds produced an apparently precise E004 interval, but the valid fold-level analysis has five units and includes zero \evd{E004}.
Likewise, the participant-averaged target in E005 supports an apparent gain, whereas the corresponding effects across nine individually evaluated participants center near zero in E008 \evd{E005} \evd{E008}.
Seeds measure optimization variability, folds measure stimulus variability, and participants measure biological variability; one axis cannot substitute for another.
E025 follows that rule by averaging seeds and folds within each person before forming its nine-participant interval \evd{E025}.

Response averaging does not estimate the same quantity as an average participant effect.
It suppresses idiosyncratic noise and emphasizes the component evoked consistently by the shared stimulus, thereby increasing reliability and changing the estimand.
The nested E010 curve suggested a dose response, but that pattern did not survive random-subset reanalysis, so it cannot carry a causal averaging law \evd{E010}.
E014 independently shows that averaging can legitimately increase an encoding score when individual signals are already positive, through higher signal-to-noise ratio and a shift toward the shared component \evd{E014}.
The positive averaged-target intervention result therefore does not establish a mean per-person effect in this cohort.
At the same time, the higher score on an averaged encoding target is a valid measurement of a different, more shared response.

\subsection{What the negative results establish}

The negative findings constrain particular mechanisms at particular inference units.
E013 establishes that the tested voxelwise MSE distillation objective does not improve held-out alignment across its weight range; it does not establish that naturalistic voxelwise objectives are impossible \evd{E013}.
E009's endpoint means favor the recorded-response arm but lack paired uncertainty; together with the unstable prerequisite representation contrast, this leaves the endpoint inconclusive and brain-specific practical utility unsupported \evd{E009}.
E024 stops before a five-arm privileged-information intervention because natural-reading gaze is uninformative about the task label and the paragraph structure cannot support the intended learning curve; it is a failed precondition, not a completed LUPI null \evd{E024}.
E020 finds that an apparent participant-residual signal is explained by stimulus leakage and autocorrelation under its strongest diagnostic, but its noisy reference prevents the experiment from closing a universal stimulus-predictability ceiling \evd{E020}.

The reduced external and cognitive-target experiments have similarly narrow scopes.
The faithful-as-feasible Negi-style head in E019 produces no positive brain-specific gain: gentle tuning barely moves the model, whereas stronger tuning damages both perplexity and encoding \evd{E019}.
Differences in architecture, dataset, training duration, and metric prevent that result from adjudicating the original Negi study; it instead corroborates the mechanism failure observed in the primary experiments.
In E021, a phase-randomized target that preserves the reading-time signal's autocorrelation and marginal shape reproduces its auxiliary-regularization advantage, so the gain is attributable to signal shape rather than cognitive content in that setup \evd{E021}.
In E022, a reduced single-participant speech experiment does not reproduce the predeclared phoneme benefit, making the planned matched-quality follow-up vacuous, but the reduced dataset and protocol do not refute the multi-participant Moussa result \evd{E022}.

\subsection{Requirements for future brain-guided training}

Future intervention studies can screen for identification failures before expensive training.
Before training, the alignment measure should be tested for leakage, against a capacity-fair nuisance model, and against an untrained network.
If response units are selected for reliability, the selection must not use the test data.
Checks for a proposed target and control should reflect how they were constructed.
Relevant examples from E026 include covariance spectrum, effective rank, baseline predictability, remaining headroom, nuisance predictability, initial loss, and gradient scale.
Equal output width is not enough.

Training success and outcome success should be reported separately.
Target uptake, parameter change, representation movement, and language quality establish that the intervention occurred.
They do not establish that the change reflects neural content.
That claim requires an adequately matched control and a common recorded-brain endpoint.
If the claim concerns people, seeds and folds must be aggregated within each participant before population inference.

A synthetic target should first be tested for held-out predictivity on the intended recorded-brain substrate under the declared nuisance and control model.
Student-side target extractability is not a substitute for that source-target validity check \evd{E030}.
A synthetic or averaged target also needs transfer beyond the target used to train it.
A direct target-trained-versus-KD contrast can test the net intervention effect even when a cross-target comparator fails, but it cannot by itself isolate which target property caused the effect.
Downstream utility should be tested only after the biological mediator is established.
This order keeps a failed manipulation, an invalid comparator, and a failed transfer endpoint from being reported as the same kind of null.

\subsection{Relation to prior positive studies}
\label{sec:discussion-prior}

Positive intervention studies answer related but different questions.
Speech studies report gains after adapting retained encoders to fMRI or ECoG targets, and Vaidya et al. test transfer from fMRI tuning to a newly fitted ECoG readout in different patients \autocite{moussa2025,zhang2026,vaidya2026}.
In text, Merlin et al. compare brain-prediction tuning with tuning on the same stimulus text, while Xiao et al. use task-fMRI-derived directions to change reasoning behavior \autocite{merlinData2026,xiao2026}.
These results show that neural objectives can be useful under their own designs.

The present experiments differ in model budget, target, comparator burden, and inference unit.
They train a smaller student, require language-quality control, audit whether the nonbrain target supports content attribution, and evaluate participant-level transfer.
Conversely, the local negative results do not reproduce the datasets, participant pools, or complete optimization procedures of the positive studies.
There is no direct contradiction: each result applies to its own intervention and endpoint.

The useful comparison is therefore between estimands rather than headline scores.
Measurement, intervention, participant generalization, and downstream payoff require different controls.
A result at one stage remains informative, but it does not inherit the claim licensed by a later stage.
