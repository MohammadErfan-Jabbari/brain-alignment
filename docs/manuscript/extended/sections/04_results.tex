\subsection{The alignment signal is real beyond low-level artifacts}

A trained model's middle layer carries unique, out-of-sample variance about real language-network activity that a same-architecture untrained network does not, and the effect survives the full low-level nuisance regression under contiguous splits.
The ROI screen passes on every model tested, and the powered voxelwise run confirms it where the screen could only hint.

The ROI screen, on the five left-hemisphere language regions of \textcite{tuckute2024} with 1000 isolated sentences over five contiguous folds, shows a positive trained-minus-untrained gap for all three models, with the untrained control negative at every layer and seed (Table~\ref{tab:roi}).
The signal is positive across a broad plateau of middle layers rather than at a single peak, and is absent from random features \evd{E002}.

The powered voxelwise run, on subject UTS03 of \textcite{lebel2023} over 20 naturalistic stories and \result{e006_reliable_voxels} voxels selected at split-half reliability above $0.5$ on a held-out repeated story, gives a trained-minus-untrained gap of \result{e006_gpt2_gap} for GPT-2 and \result{e006_qwen_gap} for Qwen2.5-0.5B, with 95\% and 99\% of reliable voxels positive respectively and the untrained unique $R^2$ at $-0.017$ in both (Table~\ref{tab:vox}) \evd{E006}.
The confidence intervals exclude zero, and the gap is carried by language training rather than by architecture or the temporal structure the nuisance floor absorbs \evd{E006}.

Three controls rule out the obvious artifacts, and the screen and the powered run are complementary on them.
The ROI screen uses isolated sentences, which removes the temporal-autocorrelation inflation that contaminates naturalistic time series, so its positive is harder to obtain rather than easier \evd{E002}.
The voxelwise run reintroduces naturalistic stimuli but adds whole-story-held-out cross-validation, so test timepoints are never adjacent to training timepoints, and voxel selection on a separate held-out repeated story, so the score cannot be inflated by voxels that correlate with the model by chance \evd{E006}.
The nuisance set subtracted in both arms is the low-level set most likely to manufacture a false positive: word and phoneme rate, word duration and length, log word-frequency, and a static word-embedding block \evd{E006}.
Surprisal and imageability are deliberately left unsubtracted, so a residual contextual nuisance is not ruled out by the floor alone; what rules it out is the same-architecture untrained network, which sees the identical set and scores negative at $-0.017$, so architecture, rate, and residual temporal structure cannot manufacture the gap \evd{E006}.

The signal is real beyond low-level artifacts on real neural data.
The three-model ROI screen carries the breadth, and the deeply-sampled single subject UTS03 carries the statistical power at voxel scale.
Cross-subject generality of the powered effect is a question this single-subject design leaves open, not one it settles.
Qwen aligns more strongly than either GPT-2 variant, while the two GPT-2 variants are nearly tied despite a threefold parameter difference, so this single cross-family contrast does not by itself establish a quality ordering; that question is taken up later \evd{E002}.
This is the foundational positive in the program: necessary but not sufficient for everything after it.
\emph{Real} does not imply \emph{movable}, and \emph{movable} does not imply \emph{useful}: this result establishes only that the signal is real.

\subsection{Plain distillation does not preserve alignment by default}

Knowledge distillation (KD) trains a smaller student to match a larger teacher's output distribution rather than to learn directly from text.
A student trained that way does not keep the teacher's brain alignment for free: ordered by how much of each representation was rebuilt through the distillation channel rather than inherited from ordinary pretraining, unique $R^2$ falls monotonically, and a from-scratch logit-KD student lands far below its teacher, a gap of \result{e003_kd_cold_delta} ($p<0.001$) at which it retains only \result{e003_kd_cold_retention} of the teacher's alignment above an untrained floor \evd{E003}.
This rules out the outcome that would have made the compression question a non-problem, preservation by default, and sizes the alignment a perplexity-only objective leaves below the teacher.
What it does not establish is the stronger claim that the distillation objective sheds alignment beyond the language-model quality it costs: across the trained and distilled models alignment tracks log-perplexity at Pearson \result{e003_quality_corr}, so quality alone could account for the whole gradient \evd{E003}\evd{L011}.

The quantity is the headroom $\Delta = A_T - A_S$ between the teacher's alignment $A_T$ and the student's $A_S$, both measured on the apparatus of the reality result: unique $R^2$ on the Tuckute ROI screen, contiguous folds, the capacity-fair nuisance floor.
The gap is an empirical question rather than a corollary, and the reason fixes the design.
For a deterministic post-processing of a fixed teacher, the quantization and pruning of \textcite{oota2026}, the student's features depend on the stimulus only through the teacher's, so the data-processing inequality bounds $I(Z_{\mathrm{student}};B)\le I(Y_{\mathrm{teacher}};B)$ and a fixed-function compression can only lose alignment, never create it; there the scientific content is how much is lost, not whether.
Distillation is not that regime: a from-scratch student fit by gradient descent on the teacher's logits has a side-channel to the stimulus through the raw next-token targets that bypasses the teacher's features, the data-processing premise fails, and nothing forces the student back to the teacher's alignment \evd{E003}.

The verdict therefore rests on a cold-init student.
A warm-started student inherits full teacher-grade alignment from its own pretraining, which the reality result already measures, so a high retention for it would show only that a few KD epochs did not erase the alignment the student already had, not that the KD channel carried it \evd{E003}.
The verdict-bearing arm is a randomly initialised GPT-2 trained on the teacher's logits alone, the one arm whose surviving alignment was transmitted through distillation; we keep a warm-init KD arm and a no-teacher plain-LM-finetune arm as labelled fine-tuning-drift and corpus-drift controls, and we score every arm at a fixed layer within the 12-layer GPT-2 architecture so no cross-architecture layer choice biases the comparison \evd{E003}.

The durable finding is the monotone gradient (Table~\ref{tab:kd}).
Retention falls cleanly along the rebuild ordering: conventional GPT-2 at the teacher's level ($\rho'\approx1.0$), warm-init KD at $0.84$, the published distilgpt2 at $0.60$, the from-scratch cold-init student at $0.37$, and the untrained floor at $0$ \evd{E003}.
The reference arms validate the measurement: off-the-shelf GPT-2 sits at the teacher's level and the untrained control at the floor, and two runs scoring the same references on different GPUs reproduced them to about $0.0003$ \evd{E003}.
The ordering holds at every PCA rank tested ($n_{\mathrm{pca}}\in\{25,50,100\}$), but the absolute shed fractions are rank-sensitive and the two quality-entangled arms flip sign across ranks, so the ordering is the finding and the magnitudes are rank-dependent \evd{E003}.

The gradient does not isolate the distillation objective from the language-model quality the student loses, because alignment co-varies with quality across the same arms.
Alignment is tightly predicted by log-perplexity across these arms (Pearson \result{e003_quality_corr}).
On that fit, warm-init KD lies essentially on the regression line, and conventional GPT-2 is the lone point above it, the only arm more aligned than its perplexity predicts.
The two contrasts that would separate an objective-specific loss from "alignment tracks quality" are both only marginal: warm-init KD is less aligned than GPT-2 despite lower perplexity, but the bootstrap $P(\text{gpt2}\le\text{kd\_warm})=0.092$, and at matched budget the no-teacher finetune beats warm-init KD on both perplexity and alignment, but $P(\text{lmft}\le\text{kd\_warm})=0.085$; each leans on a single unreplicated point and, under a paired-fold test, on one fold carrying about $44\%$ of the signal \evd{E003}\evd{L011}.
The cold-init arm compounds the difficulty with under-training, its held-out perplexity ($477$) sitting far above the converged arms' ($44$ to $95$), so part of its near-floor alignment is simply a worse language model rather than distillation shedding alignment, the convergence caveat the design predeclared \evd{E003}.
The match across arms was the compute budget, not the optimizer-step count: the from-scratch arm ran about twice the steps of the warm arms, which deepens this caveat rather than flattering it \evd{E003}.

Plain perplexity-only distillation does not preserve brain alignment by default, and the headroom below the teacher is genuine but entangled with model quality.
Whether an alignment-guided objective can recover that headroom beyond what perplexity already implies is the matched-perplexity question the rest of the program takes up, not one this screen settles.
The benchmark here is a cheap ROI screen ($\mathrm{NC}\approx0.35$) at matched compute budget, under-powered to resolve a $\sim0.005$ objective-specific gap and not run at matched perplexity, which is why that question moves to the powered voxelwise substrate \evd{E003}\evd{L011}.
