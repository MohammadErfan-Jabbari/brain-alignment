\section{Estimators, Inference, and Formal Scope}
\label{app:inference}

This appendix separates three questions that are easy to conflate: what each encoding score predicts, which observations support its uncertainty statement, and which formal analogies apply.
The empirical quantities are cross-validated predictive contrasts.
They are neither causal effects nor direct estimates of information-theoretic objects.

\subsection{Encoding estimators}

Section~\ref{sec:measurement-prerequisites} defines ordinary held-out \(R^2\) and the cross-validated semipartial increment in Equations~\ref{eq:heldout-r2} and~\ref{eq:semipartial-r2}.
The numerator of Equation~\ref{eq:heldout-r2} is the held-out sum of squared errors, \(\mathrm{SSE}_j(M)\), and its denominator is the held-out total sum of squares, \(\mathrm{SST}_j\).
Equation~\ref{eq:semipartial-r2} measures the change in held-out linear predictive value when \(X\) is added to the specified nuisance design.
Because the nuisance-only and full ridge estimators are separately regularized and evaluated out of sample, the difference can be negative.
It is not a nonnegative variance decomposition.\evd{E002}\evd{E006}

For comparison, let \(\mathcal R_{N,j}^2\) and \(\mathcal R_{F,j}^2\) denote the population coefficients of determination for the nested, unregularized linear projections on \(N\) and \([N,X]\).
The corresponding partial coefficient is
\begin{equation}
R_{\mathrm{partial},j}^2
=
\frac{\mathcal R_{F,j}^2-\mathcal R_{N,j}^2}{1-\mathcal R_{N,j}^2}
=
1-
\frac{\operatorname{Var}(Y_j-\Pi_{[N,X]}Y_j)}
{\operatorname{Var}(Y_j-\Pi_NY_j)},
\label{eq:partial-r2}
\end{equation}
where \(\Pi\) denotes the corresponding population linear projection and \(\mathcal R_{N,j}^2<1\).
In a common-sample least-squares fit, the variance ratio is replaced by \(\mathrm{SSE}_{F,j}/\mathrm{SSE}_{N,j}\).
Equation~\ref{eq:partial-r2} asks what fraction of the variance left by \(N\) is removed by \(X\); Equation~\ref{eq:semipartial-r2} asks how much total outcome variance is gained.
The thesis reports Equation~\ref{eq:semipartial-r2}, not a transformed cross-validated partial coefficient.

Most recorded-response assays first compute Equation~\ref{eq:heldout-r2} per \ac{roi} or voxel, subtract reduced from full within that coordinate and fold, and then average the declared coordinates and folds equally.
The continuity target-recovery assay also averages coordinatewise target \(R^2\).
The exact-substrate target-recovery assay is the exception: it sums \(\mathrm{SSE}_j\) and \(\mathrm{SST}_j\) across all 20,484 fixed target coordinates before taking \(1-\mathrm{SSE}/\mathrm{SST}\).
That estimator weights coordinates by held-out variance and prevents near-constant coordinates from receiving the same weight as variable coordinates.
Neither aggregation treats coordinates as independent repetitions. \evd{E016}\evd{E030}

Ridge regression supplies the fitted conditional mean.
After train-fold centering and predictor standardization, suppose
\[
y=X\beta+\varepsilon,\qquad
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2 I),
\qquad
\beta\sim\mathcal N(0,\tau_\beta^2 I).
\]
The posterior mode minimizes
\[
\|y-X\beta\|_2^2+\lambda\|\beta\|_2^2,
\qquad
\lambda=\sigma^2/\tau_\beta^2.
\]
This is a maximum-a-posteriori estimator only under that Gaussian likelihood, an isotropic zero-mean coefficient prior, and the stated scaling convention.
The implementations choose \(\lambda\) from a finite grid using training data, which is better understood operationally as predictive regularization than as a fixed scientific prior.
The held-out \(R^2\) then evaluates a predictive algorithm.
Neither the Gaussian derivation nor a positive score makes the fitted coefficients causal, identifies a neural mechanism, or implies that model and brain implement the same computation.

\subsection{Cross-validation and inference units}

Every learned operation that can use stimulus-domain information is fitted on the relevant training complement.
For each complete naturalistic story, fixed feature resampling and edge slicing, within-story feature and \ac{bold} z-scoring, and finite-impulse-response feature-delay copies are constructed separately before cross-validation.
Only train-complement \ac{pca}, assembled-design scaling, response centering, ridge selection, and readout fitting are learned for that assay.
The sole declared exception is the training-target standardization reused as a frozen external object in the exact-substrate diagnostic; subsequent target \ac{pca} remains fold-local.

Regularization selection is nested inside each fitting set.
The baseline sentence and naturalistic story assays run \texttt{RidgeCV} on the relevant complement for readout training and share one selected penalty across outputs.
Recorded-target fresh readouts repeat that selection inside each inner training subfold.
The exact-substrate diagnostic likewise forbids per-coordinate penalty selection: each aligned or twin multi-output readout selects one scalar penalty on its own training complement, while the paired paths reuse the train-fold target-\ac{pca} basis.
Candidate layer grids, nuisance tiers, \ac{pca} ranks, reliability thresholds, participants, and \acp{roi} are declared before scoring.
The baseline screen reports best layers descriptively within its fixed grid; later recorded-response assays prospectively reuse those screen choices.
The exact-substrate target-retention and composed student-to-target-to-response assays fix the trained layer~6 as primary and layer~7 as a locked, non-rescuing sensitivity. \evd{E002}\evd{E006}\evd{E025}\evd{E030}

The nesting order determines what can generalize.
Repeated seeds vary optimizer randomness under one fixed procedure.
Stimulus folds vary held-out blocks but share observations and have overlapping training complements.
Voxels share one brain, spatial dependence, preprocessing, and stimuli.
Target coordinates share the same generated target and student.
None can replace a participant when the claim concerns people.
Conversely, participants viewed through one fixed 1,000-sentence set do not by themselves establish stimulus-population generalization.
The participant recorded-target assay therefore reports both participant and shared-fold robustness; saved-student transfer and exact-substrate response assays aggregate folds and seeds before participant inference; the naturalistic measurement retracts its voxel-bootstrap interval and keeps its within-person point contrast and story-block signs. \evd{E006}\evd{E008}\evd{E025}\evd{E030}

\subsection{What information theory does and does not imply}

\paragraph{Conditional information and partial variance.}
For scalar \(Y\), jointly Gaussian \((X,Y,Z)\), correctly specified population-linear conditional means, and unregularized population residual variances,
\begin{equation}
I(X;Y\mid Z)
=
-\frac12\log\!\left(1-R_{\mathrm{partial}}^2\right),
\qquad
\Delta \mathcal R_{\mathrm{semi}}^2
=
(1-\mathcal R_N^2)R_{\mathrm{partial}}^2.
\label{eq:gaussian-cmi-r2}
\end{equation}
The multivariate analogue replaces the scalar residual-variance ratio with a log-determinant ratio.
These are distributional identities under the stated Gaussian model; the thesis instead estimates finite-sample, cross-validated semipartial differences from separately regularized ridge fits \autocite{cover2006}.
Those differences may be negative and depend on the nuisance design, rank, penalty, and held-out substrate; they are operational incremental-prediction estimates, not estimates or lower bounds of \(I(X;Y\mid Z)\).

\paragraph{Data processing.}
The data-processing inequality would let a predicted target \(T\) upper-bound what a trained state \(H\) retains about a recorded response \(B\) only under \(B\to T\to H\), equivalently \(B\perp H\mid T\), which implies \(I(B;H)\leq I(B;T)\).
Direct teacher compression analogously requires \(B\to H_T\to H_S\).
Neither chain follows from the objective: TRIBE, the teacher, and the student share text-derived information, and the student has a direct stimulus/corpus path not mediated by the target or teacher state.
Conditioning on the stimulus does not restore the chain when the target is a deterministic or nearly deterministic function of that stimulus.
Data processing therefore motivates a preservation test only after conditional independence is defended; it does not predict the sign of a cross-validated encoding contrast \autocite{cover2006}. \evd{E003}\evd{E016}\evd{E030}

\paragraph{Generalization and rate--distortion.}
Let \(Z^n\) be an i.i.d. sample and let a learning algorithm map it, possibly randomly, to parameters \(W\).
If the loss \(\ell(w,Z)\) is \(\sigma\)-sub-Gaussian under the population distribution for every fixed \(w\), and \(L(W)\) and \(\widehat L(W)\) denote population and empirical risk, then expectation over the sample and algorithm randomness gives
\[
\left|\mathbb E[L(W)-\widehat L(W)]\right|
\leq
\sqrt{\frac{2\sigma^2 I(W;Z^n)}{n}}
\]
This information-theoretic generalization bound \autocite{xu2017} grows with algorithm--sample information, which this experiment does not estimate.
The bound is therefore an organizing constraint, not an explanation of an observed result: it neither guarantees that a neural target improves generalization nor makes the benefit scale with a ``brain-information budget.''

Classical rate--distortion theory defines
\[
R(D)
=
\min_{P_{\widehat S\mid S}:\,\mathbb E[d(S,\widehat S)]\leq D}
I(S;\widehat S)
\]
In this classical object \autocite{cover2006}, parameter count, optimizer steps, and perplexity are not the mutual-information rate, and the target loss is not established as a source-coding distortion.
The manuscript therefore uses ``rate--distortion-style'' only for the empirical Pareto relation among model budget, language quality, and controlled predictivity.
No theorem about \(R(D)\) guarantees that neural supervision moves that frontier favorably.

\paragraph{Output matching and internal alignment.}
Equation~\ref{eq:implemented-kd} constrains the student's output distribution, not internal-state geometry, because many parameterizations realize the same next-token distribution:
\[
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\!\left(p_T^\tau\|q_S^\tau\right)\to0
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
H_S\approx H_T
\;\not\Rightarrow\;
\Delta R^2(H_S\to B)=\Delta R^2(H_T\to B).
\]
The first non-implication is architectural; the second also depends on the evaluation target, nuisance model, and readout.
Language quality must therefore be measured, but a matched output score cannot itself certify biological alignment. \evd{E003}\evd{E015}

Low auxiliary target loss is no stronger: a temporary head may absorb it, or a nuisance-predictable target may be learned without a participant-general incremental relation to recorded responses.
Held-out target uptake and retained-student movement show learning and change, not neural-content attribution or biological transfer without a valid comparator and participant endpoint.
In canonical stage order, exact-target practical linear measurability fails its continuation rule; target uptake and retained-student movement pass; brain-response-specific attribution remains unsupported because the comparator is non-identifying; and biological transfer is near zero under the tested assay. \evd{E030}\evd{E016}\evd{E026}\evd{E025}
