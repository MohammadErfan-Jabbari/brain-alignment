\title{From Brain Scores to Training Signals: A Controlled Test of Neural-Guided Distillation}
\author{MohammadErfan Jabbari}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Linear mappings from language-model representations to brain activity reveal decodable neural signal but not whether it is useful for training. We ask one governing question: can this signal produce a participant-general, brain-specific benefit when used to guide language-model distillation? Our design separates training on recorded functional magnetic resonance imaging responses from training on dense neural proxies predicted from text. Middle-layer representations predicted recorded responses after nuisance controls. Compression left a teacher-to-student predictivity gap, but the student also had worse language quality, so the source of that intervention opportunity remained unresolved. The recorded-target branch did not support a participant-general gain. In the predicted-target branch, the exact target first failed a fixed practical test: it did not add enough linear prediction of recorded responses beyond nuisance features. A saved training run nevertheless showed that the proxy became easier to recover from the student and that the student's deployed representations changed without unacceptable language-quality loss. A fresh assay did not establish that the trained student retained more of the target than ordinary distillation. The available nonbrain target was not matched well enough to isolate neural content, and direct biological transfer across nine participants remained near zero. Taken together, the results show measurable neural signal and successful manipulation, but no demonstrated participant-general, brain-specific training benefit in the tested systems. This localizes where the evidence chain failed rather than proving a universal null.
\end{abstract}
