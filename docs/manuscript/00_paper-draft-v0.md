# A brain-tuning gain that vanishes per individual: cross-subject-averaged fMRI targets inflate apparent alignment-specificity at matched perplexity

*Working draft (v0.9), 2026-06-12.* Contribution: an empirical measurement-validity result — a brain-tuning gain measured against a cross-subject-averaged fMRI target need not reflect alignment to any individual brain — with a confound-clean validation protocol (matched-perplexity + permuted-fMRI twin + per-individual inference). All reported numbers trace to a recorded experiment (`docs/experiments/ENNN`) or learning (`docs/learnings.md` Lnnn), cited inline; this provenance note is for the internal repo and would be dropped at submission.

---

## Abstract

On a benchmark whose standard target is cross-subject-*averaged* (Tuckute 2024), an alignment-guided distillation objective produces a +0.008 "brain-specific" gain that **passes** both a permuted-fMRI-twin control and matched perplexity — yet is a **well-powered zero in every one of 9 individual brains** (mean +0.0001, 95% CI [−0.0004, +0.0006]; a sensitivity simulation gives power 1.0 to detect a per-individual effect a fifth of that size, so this is a true null, not low power). The averaged-target gain is alignment to the *shared stimulus-evoked component* — which averaging amplifies at an inflated noise ceiling — not to any person, and it vanishes at the individual-subject inference unit. We therefore report a measurement-validity result: **a brain-tuning gain measured against a cross-subject-averaged fMRI target need not reflect alignment to any individual brain**, which implicates optimization claims evaluated against averaged-target benchmarks (Tuckute; group-average RSA). This is sharper than prior work finding averaged targets merely *suboptimal* (Moussa 2025) — the per-individual reality is zero, so the gain is artifactual, not attenuated. We separately confirm that LM-to-brain alignment is real and powerfully measurable in aggregate (trained−untrained voxelwise gap +0.021–0.028 on LeBel, after a strong nuisance regression; `E006`) — measuring it and optimizing it are different. The operative corrective is per-individual inference (the permuted twin and matched perplexity were both fooled); with matched perplexity — the load-bearing control no prior brain-tuning study includes — these form a validation protocol we recommend as method. That averaging raises the noise ceiling is known fMRI methods (Lage-Castellanos 2019; Nili 2014); the consequence for optimization-claim validity is ours. Nulls hold in a matched-perplexity distillation regime, robust to LoRA capacity *and* to a contrastive (NT-Xent) objective (both per-individual null); the one untested door is a different *induction method* — full fine-tuning on multi-subject naturalistic voxelwise targets (the voxelwise distillation lever itself fails to take hold).

## 1. Introduction

Two facts are by now well established. First, representations in the middle layers of LLMs predict human brain activity during language tasks via a simple linear encoding model, and the predictivity rises with model quality (the "brain score" literature). Second, much of that predictivity is explained by low-level and static features, and naive evaluation (shuffled train/test splits, no nuisance regression) badly inflates it — the residual attributable to a *trained* contextual model is "real but small," on the order of ≤10% of explainable variance (Hadidi/Feghhi 2026; `docs/literature/canonical/hadidi-2024`).

The natural next question — and the one a recent line of work answers optimistically — is whether the mapping can be *used*: can we optimize a model to be more brain-aligned and thereby get a better model (more robust, more sample-efficient, better-compressed at matched budget)? This is the thesis's framing (`docs/00-charter.md`): brain-alignment as a *usable training signal*, with knowledge distillation as the first concrete use case (does an alignment-guided student preserve brain-relevant structure, and its utility, better than a brain-blind student at the same budget?).

Our contribution is an **empirical measurement-validity result**: a brain-tuning gain reported against a cross-subject-*averaged* fMRI target need not reflect alignment to any individual brain. The same alignment-guided distillation objective gives a +0.008 "brain-specific" gain against a 5-subject-averaged target — and that gain *passes* a permuted-fMRI-twin control and is at matched perplexity — yet is a **well-powered null against every individual brain** (+0.0001, n=9; the test has power 1.0 to detect a per-individual effect of the averaged magnitude, so this is a true null, not low power). Averaging amplifies the *shared stimulus-evoked component* at an inflated noise ceiling; the gain is alignment to that shared component, not to any person, and it vanishes at the individual-subject inference unit. Averaged targets are not universal practice — the per-subject encoding and brain-tuning lineage fits individuals — but they are the practice of the widely-used Tuckute (2024) benchmark we optimize against and of group-average RSA, so the result invalidates a class of optimization claims measured against such targets. The corrective is the one control that caught it — **per-individual inference** (the permuted twin and matched-perplexity baseline were both passed by the artifact); we recommend it, with matched-perplexity, as a validation protocol (method, §3), but the contribution is the empirical finding, not the protocol.

**Claims, and how we control them.** Throughout, the unit of a brain-alignment claim is the individual subject; the null is a *per-kind permuted-fMRI twin* trained under the identical objective (so it is matched on perplexity by construction); and any "beyond perplexity" comparison is made at matched perplexity, because alignment co-varies strongly with LM quality (Pearson r≈−0.88 against log-perplexity; `L011`). These three controls — per-subject inference, the permuted twin, and matched perplexity — are the protocol.

## 2. Related work

**Brain-alignment as a measurement.** Trained LMs predict language-network activity above untrained controls; alignment tracks scale and quality. Anti-confound work (Feghhi/Hadidi 2026, `hadidi-2024`; Oota 2024, `oota-2024`) shows positional/word-rate/static features explain most predictivity and that contiguous splits and nuisance regression are mandatory; the trained residual is ≤10%. Input-attribution work (Proietti 2025, `proietti-2025`) reports that brain-alignment and next-word prediction draw on *distinct* word subsets (IoU≈0.16), a mechanistic basis for a "beyond perplexity" component — though on frozen models, without the Hadidi nuisance bar. Merlin & Toneva 2024 (`merlin-2024`) find a residual alignment component surviving word-level and next-word-prediction controls.

**Brain-alignment as a training signal (the claim we test).** Schwartz, Toneva & Wehbe 2019 (`schwartz-2019`) fine-tune BERT on fMRI/MEG and report GLUE is "not harmed" (the founding result; no matched-perplexity or shuffled-brain control). Negi et al. 2025 (`negi-2025`) brain-tune multilingual models and report downstream gains (+0.5–1.6pp), but baseline against a *vanilla pretrained* model — not perplexity-matched, no shuffled-brain downstream null, no compression. Moussa et al. 2025 (`moussa-2025`) report speech-LM gains. In vision, Li/Brendel 2019 regularize CNNs with neural RSA and improve robustness — but Pirlot 2022 (`pirlot-2022`) shows the *accuracy* gain is reproduced by a shuffled-label control and only the *robustness* gain needs real neural structure, and Hoak 2025 shows *aggregate* alignment does not predict robustness (only feature-specific does); Guo 2024 finds EEG co-training robustness gains are "limited but consistent" (~2–4pp). The common gap: **none of the text-LM positives control for perplexity or include a brain-specificity null.** That is exactly the control our protocol adds, and the axis on which we find the effect vanishes.

**Compression context.** Oota 2026 (`oota-2026`) shows benchmark performance and alignment partially dissociate under post-hoc quantization/pruning, but never tests distillation — *shrink × measure*, not *shrink × optimize*. The alignment-guided-distillation-at-matched-budget question was open; we close it (negatively).

**Targets: per-subject vs cross-subject-averaged (the locus of our result).** We surveyed how the field constructs its fMRI target. The dominant encoding lineage (Schrimpf 2021, Caucheteux & King 2022, Antonello & Huth 2023) and *every* brain-tuning study we found (Schwartz 2019, Negi 2025, Bilgin 2026, Moussa 2025a/b, Aw & Toneva 2023) fits **per-subject** (scores aggregated afterward) — these are *not* touched by our finding. Cross-subject *target averaging* before fitting is used by (i) the **Tuckute 2024** controlled-sentence benchmark we optimize against, and (ii) **group-average RSA** (Nili 2014). Our contribution is thus not a refutation of a specific published *tuning* result (none we found averages targets) but a **validity warning latent in averaged-target benchmarks**: any optimization or alignment claim evaluated against such a target inherits the inflation we demonstrate, and we show the inflated signal is *per-individually absent*. As brain-tuning increasingly adopts shared/averaged targets for data efficiency, this is the regime where the warning bites.

## 3. Methods

**Datasets.** (i) *Tuckute 2024* sentence-level fMRI (5 LH language sub-ROIs), 1000 isolated sentences, per-participant responses for 10 participants; isolated sentences sidestep the temporal-autocorrelation leakage of naturalistic data. **Its standard target is participant-averaged** — Tuckute fit their encoding model to BOLD "averaged within each train participant and averaged across participants to yield an average language network response to each of the 1,000 baseline set sentences" (this is the released benchmark target, and what our loader reconstructs: a mean over the train-UID responses). This averaged target is the object our central result is about; we additionally hold out each participant individually. (ii) *LeBel UTS03* voxelwise naturalistic listening fMRI (deep within-subject sampling), used for the powered A2 measurement. Noise ceiling for the Tuckute targets, matched to the five functional sub-ROIs, is NC≈0.491 (`E004`/`E008`).

**Encoding metric.** Per-voxel/ROI ridge encoding R², and **unique R²** = R²([nuisance, LM-features]) − R²([nuisance]) — the conditional-mutual-information estimand (`docs/06-theory-grounding.md` §3). The verdict statistic is the *trained−untrained gap* (an untrained same-architecture net can score positive via temporal/rate structure; the gap is the normaliser-invariant signal). Nuisance (subtracted identically from both arms) is the strong low-level set: word-rate, phoneme-rate, word-duration, word-length, log-frequency, and static embeddings — Feghhi's headline confounds. Splits are contiguous (sentence folds; story-level CV for LeBel).

**Powered A2 (E006).** LeBel UTS03, ~20 stories, story-grouped CV, per-word contextual LM features → Lanczos→TR → FIR(1–4); NC-reliable voxels selected on a *held-out* repeated story (split-half reliability >0.5; no double-dipping). gpt2 (L7) and Qwen2.5-0.5B (L12) vs untrained same-architecture controls (≥3 seeds).

**The alignment-guided distillation objective.** Student Qwen2.5-0.5B distilled from teacher Qwen2.5-1.5B by KD (temperature-scaled KL on next-token logits, the perplexity-retention term) plus λ·MSE from the student's verdict-layer pooled hidden state to the fMRI target (a co-trained linear readout — the theory-preferred Gaussian lower bound on conditional MI, `D016`). LoRA adapters (base frozen → perplexity stays near the teacher's; full fine-tuning collapses perplexity, `E004`). Input embeddings frozen; static nuisance from the untrained base, byte-identical across arms.

**Arms and the controls.** `kd_ppl` = KD only (the matched-perplexity baseline). `kd_brain` = KD + brain MSE. `kd_brain_permuted` = KD + MSE toward a block-permuted fMRI target (the brain-specificity null; matched on perplexity by construction since it shares the objective). For the practical test we add `kd_textfeat` = KD + MSE toward a *text-feature-predicted* target (ridge[imageability, GPT2-XL surprisal, PCFG surprisal] → BOLD), separating "brain-derived" from "any smooth text-correlated regressor."

**Inference (the load-bearing choice).** The independent replication unit is the **subject**. The per-subject effect e_u is the median over folds×seeds of (uR²(kd_brain) − uR²(kd_brain_permuted)) on held-out sentences. We report the across-subject mean with a t-CI and a sign test, *and* — because all subjects see the same sentences on the same folds (shared-stimulus correlation) — a conservative fold-clustered CI with leave-one-fold-out, taking the more conservative as the headline (`E008` design; oracle-gated). We split the 9 usable Tuckute participants (a 10th, uid 853, excluded for incomplete ROI coverage) into the **4** averaged in prior work (train) vs the 5 held-out (4+5=9), and we report a per-subject-SNR control. Minimum detectable effects (MDEs) are *measured* from the run's own seed/fold variance, never borrowed.

## 4. Results

### 4.1 Alignment is real and measurable (A2, powered) — `E006`

On LeBel UTS03 voxelwise, after the full low-level nuisance regression and story-level CV, the trained−untrained unique-R² gap on 11,442 NC-reliable voxels is **+0.0207 [95% CI +0.0205, +0.0209] for gpt2 (L7)** and **+0.0277 [+0.0274, +0.0280] for Qwen2.5-0.5B (L12)**, with 95% and 99% of reliable voxels positive respectively. The *gap* is the verdict statistic because an untrained net scores *negative* out-of-sample after nuisance (−0.0169 gpt2 / −0.0173 Qwen); the trained *absolute* unique R² is modest (+0.0038 gpt2, +0.0103 Qwen ≈ 2% of NC≈0.49) — so the honest reading is "a trained LM predicts brain activity reliably *above an untrained control*," not "explains a large share of brain variance." Qwen>gpt2 (the expected quality ordering); clears the Hadidi/Feghhi bar. **The signal exists.**

### 4.2 The apparent optimization gain — and that it lives only in the averaged target — `E005`→`E008` (the central result)

Against a **5-subject-averaged** Tuckute target, the alignment-guided student beats its matched-perplexity permuted twin: paired (kd_brain − kd_brain_permuted) = **+0.0081**, with a 15-fold×seed bootstrap CI [+0.0023, +0.0171] that excludes zero (`E005`). Taken at face value this is "alignment-guided KD recovers brain-specific alignment beyond perplexity."

It does not survive its controls. **(a) Inference unit.** The 15 cells are 3 seeds × 5 folds over *one* averaged target on the same 1000 sentences — pseudo-replication. At the honest unit (5 folds), the t-CI is **[−0.0030, +0.0193] (includes 0)**; one fold (fold-4/seed-0, paired diff +0.0636) contributes 52% of the signal; the median is +0.0034 (`L015`). **(b) Per-subject.** Running the identical contrast per individual (n=9), the effect collapses to **mean +0.00010, t-CI [−0.00037, +0.00058], sign 5/9 (p=0.50); the conservative fold-clustered CI includes 0 and fails leave-one-fold-out** (`E008`). The four subjects that were averaged in the prior result are individually null (mean −0.00010). The per-subject MDE(80%) is **+0.0006–0.0013** — six to twelve times *below* the +0.0081 we are testing — so this is a **well-powered null**, not a noise-floor null: an effect a fifth the claimed size would have been detected.

**Why the averaged target shows a gap the individual doesn't (one mechanism).** Write subject BOLD as Y_i = g + ε_i, with g the shared stimulus-evoked response and ε_i idiosyncratic + measurement noise (independent across subjects). Averaging k subjects preserves g and shrinks the noise ~k×, so tuning toward the average is tuning toward **g** — the shared, largely stimulus-driven component — at an inflated noise ceiling. The unique-R² gain read off the averaged target is therefore a gain in predicting *g*, and it reads as "brain-specific" against a permuted twin (which destroys g's stimulus correspondence) even though **no individual's own Y_i carries it** (E008: per-subject ~0). This is *not* a small real per-subject effect amplified ~1.7×: the noise-ceiling inflation at NC≈0.49 is only Var(Y_i)/Var(Ȳ)=1/(0.49+0.51/5)≈**1.7×**, which a true per-subject effect of ~+0.002 would require — but the per-subject effect is +0.0001 (well-powered), so the +0.008 averaged number is dominated by *alignment to the shared component g* (plus, in E005 specifically, ~2.4× inflation from one outlier fold and 15-cell pseudo-replication, L015). The 1.7× is thus a *lower bound on ceiling inflation*, not the explanation; the explanation is that averaging changes the **estimand** — from "per-person alignment" to "alignment to the group-shared stimulus response." And even that shared-component gain is only "specific" relative to an incomplete nuisance set (⅓–⅔ of the apparent unique R² co-varies with imageability/surprisal the static nuisance never subtracts, `L012`).

**(b) The averaging dose-response — `E010`.** Two facts establish that the gap is a property of the *averaged target*, not of individuals. First, **the k=1 (single-subject) gap is null for all 9 individuals** (this is exactly `E008`: per-subject mean +0.0001, none positive beyond noise) — so the gap genuinely vanishes without averaging. Second, varying the number of subjects k averaged (nested subsets, k∈{1,2,3,5,9}, 4 seeds), the held-out gap is **−0.0002 / −0.0001 / +0.0023 / +0.0194 / +0.0071** for k=1/2/3/5/9 — it appears and grows once subjects are averaged, with the rising limb (k=1→5) tracking the predicted ceiling NC_k = NC/(NC+(1−NC)/k) (0.49→0.83). **Honest caveat (the nested design confounds subject-count with subject-identity):** the nested subsets add specific subjects at each k, so part of the rise could reflect *which* subjects entered rather than *how many* (and the curve is not monotone — k=9<k=5, within wide error bars). We therefore re-tested the rise with **random size-k subsets** (`E010b`): the clean curve does **not** survive — gap(k) = +0.0015 / −0.0013 / +0.0088 / +0.0094 for k=1/3/5/9 is non-monotone and very noisy (sd 0.005–0.015 at 2 seeds × 3 subsets/k), and the 875-in/out split is inconsistent (uid 875 alone gives +0.0038 at k=1, yet at k=5 the 875-*out* subsets give the larger gap, +0.0125). So the **monotone dose-response was partly a nested-design artifact**, and we do **not** claim a clean causal dose-response law (`L020`). What is solid stands on firmer ground that needs no dose-response: the **averaged target gives +0.008 (`E005`) while the per-individual reality is ~0 (`E008`, n=9, well-powered)** — the averaged measurement reports a gap the individual does not have. That contrast *is* the inflation; the dose-response is at most suggestive corroboration (averaged targets at high k tend to show a gap, consistent with the noise-ceiling mechanism, but noisily).

### 4.3 No per-individual optimizable gain (F1) — `E008`

Consequently, **optimizing brain-alignment yields no detectable per-individual brain-specific gain beyond perplexity.** The in-domain "headline" of alignment-guided distillation is an averaging artifact.

### 4.4 No practical payoff, and the manipulation does not take hold (A3) — `E009`

We test whether the alignment-guided student is practically better — out-of-domain perplexity robustness (WikiText→Pereira and →LeBel-spoken transcripts; ratio of OOD to in-domain perplexity), with `kd_ppl`, `kd_brain_permuted`, and `kd_textfeat` controls, at matched perplexity, n=8 seeds. Two findings. **(i) The fulcrum is ~0 at matched perplexity:** the brain-specific representational gap (kd_brain − kd_brain_permuted held-out unique R²) is median +0.004 (mean +0.011 but seed-0-outlier-driven, +0.063 vs ~+0.004 for the other seven; MDE 0.023 ≈ the mean) — no reliable brain-specific change is induced, and raising λ to grow it collapses perplexity (λ=30 → ppl 92 vs base 45, one seed 148). **(ii) No brain-specific downstream effect:** every OOD-ratio contrast (kd_brain vs kd_ppl, vs permuted, vs textfeat) lies within the measured MDE (0.03–0.13). A3 is a **bounded null, null-by-construction**: with no reliable manipulation at matched perplexity there is nothing whose downstream value to test.

## 5. Discussion

**The positive contribution — and its precise scope.** The methodological finding has two distinct parts that must not be conflated. (i) *Cross-subject fMRI target averaging inflates apparent brain-specificity in model optimization* — established by the contrast that the same objective gives +0.008 against a 5-subject-averaged target (E005) but ~0 per individual (E008, n=9, well-powered; the subjects-averaged dose-response §4.2b is consistent but too noisy to add a clean causal law). **Novelty scoping (grounded):** that averaging *raises the achievable noise ceiling* by suppressing per-subject noise is **known fMRI methods** — formally for within-subject repetitions (Lage-Castellanos 2019; the cross-subject extension and the ρ_NC(k)=√(σ²_g/(σ²_g+σ²_ε/k)) form in §4.2 are our application) and in RSA group-ceiling practice (Nili 2014). Our novelty is therefore **not the mechanism** but its **consequence for the validity of brain-*tuning* claims**: a distillation/fine-tuning gain reported against an averaged target can be an averaging artifact that no individual exhibits. The closest prior, Moussa (NeurIPS 2025), independently *avoids* averaging (per-participant gradient steps beat averaged targets) — corroborating that averaging matters — but frames it as a data-efficiency design choice (averaged targets are *suboptimal*). **Our increment is sharper: the per-individual reality is a well-powered *zero*** (power 1.0 to detect a +0.003 per-individual effect; sensitivity sim, `scripts/sensitivity_e008.py`), so the averaged gain is *artifactual*, not merely attenuated. This critique applies only to studies that **average targets across subjects** (Tuckute; group-average RSA), not the per-subject lineage. (ii) **Which control caught it (stated honestly):** the averaged +0.008 *passed* both the permuted-fMRI twin (it beat it) and matched-perplexity (matched by construction) — only **per-individual inference** exposed it. So the operative corrective is subject-level inference; the *matched-perplexity baseline* + permuted twin remain necessary in general to separate brain-specific from LM-quality gains (alignment co-varies with perplexity, r≈−0.88, `L011`) — and matched-perplexity is the load-bearing control no prior brain-tuning study includes (Moussa/Negi have the permuted twin and per-individual, but not matched-perplexity). We recommend all three as a protocol (§3), while being clear the headline contribution is the empirical finding, not the protocol.

**Honest reconciliation with Negi 2025 (a positive prior).** Negi report downstream NLP gains *and* a per-participant, TR-shuffle-controlled, positive *encoding* gain. The averaging critique (i) does **not** reach them — they tune per participant, not on an average. Their *downstream* claim is exposed to critique (ii) (vanilla, non-perplexity-matched baseline). But their *encoding* result is a per-individual brain-specificity-controlled positive that our `E008` null appears to contradict — and we must be honest that **our null is regime-specific**: light LoRA on ~800 isolated sentences with a frozen base to hold perplexity, vs Negi's full fine-tuning / contrastive objective / naturalistic data. The matched-perplexity constraint may itself suppress the effect (§4.4: λ cannot grow the representational change without collapsing perplexity). So our claim is not "brain-tuning never works per individual"; it is "**under a matched-perplexity distillation regime on language ROIs, no per-individual brain-specific gain is detectable, and the averaged-target gain that motivated it is an averaging artifact.**" We tested the tuning-intensity escape (E011, heavy LoRA) — the null holds, and the matched-perplexity constraint bounds how hard the representation can be pushed (§6). Directly rebutting Negi's *encoding* result would require matching their objective and data (full-FT + contrastive loss + naturalistic narrative), a different paradigm we flag as the honest open door (§6) — not the tuning intensity E011 already ruled out.

**Why this is consistent, not contradictory.** Alignment is real (§4.1) and yet not a usable per-individual optimization lever (§4.3–4.4). There is no contradiction: a signal can be robustly *measurable* in aggregate and still be (i) dominated by a shared stimulus-evoked component that averaging amplifies, (ii) ≤10% trained residual (Hadidi/Feghhi), and (iii) too small and idiosyncratic per individual to optimize at matched perplexity. Measuring ≠ optimizing.

**Relation to theory.** The unique-R² estimand is the conditional MI of layer features and brain given nuisance (`06-theory-grounding.md` §3); averaging changes the target's noise ceiling, so effect-on-averaged-target and mean-of-per-subject-effects are different estimands and need not agree — the per-subject one is what "generalizes across people" means. The data-processing inequality is not violated: at inference the student sees only text, so any brain-relevant structure is bounded by the stimulus, and the brain term at best *reformats* stimulus-derived information into linearly-decodable directions rather than creating brain information.

## 6. Limitations

(1) **Operationalization of "practical."** A3's payoff axis is out-of-domain perplexity (offline constraint); the perturbation-robustness-slope and sample-efficiency axes that the vision literature finds brain-specific (Pirlot/Hoak) were not run — but with the fulcrum at ~0 (§4.4), any downstream test is uninformative, so this bounds rather than reopens the claim. (2) **Scale/scope:** one student/teacher (Qwen 0.5B←1.5B), Tuckute language ROIs + LeBel UTS03; the per-subject null is established for this regime. (3) **The only grounded path to a per-individual positive** is higher per-subject SNR (within-subject fMRI repeats to lift the single-subject ceiling) or conditioning surprisal/imageability into the nuisance — not more cross-subject averaging. (4) The averaged-target residual (+0.003–0.004, non-significant) is itself partly an unsubtracted-nuisance artifact (`L012`); we do not claim it is a clean brain effect. (5) **Substrate mismatch — attempted; the distillation lever does not take hold.** The positive A2 is on LeBel voxelwise, the optimization null on Tuckute ROIs, so we attempted the same-substrate test: a voxelwise per-individual tuning loop on LeBel (UTS01/02/03; `E013`). Across λ∈{1,3,6,10} the voxelwise brain-MSE lever never *improves* held-out-story alignment over the untuned base (neutral at low λ — real≈base; monotonically degrading above) and never beats its permuted twin (gap ≤0 throughout). So this *distillation-readout* lever cannot be made to take hold on the powered substrate — visible on a single subject across all λ, i.e. a **mechanism failure, not a power limit** (more deep subjects cannot rescue a manipulation that fails on one). It reinforces the per-individual null — the objective never even improves held-out alignment here — but is not a clean same-substrate null, which would require a *different* induction method that first improves alignment (see open door, §6(6)). (Full v1/v2 trace in `E013`/`L026`/`L027`.) (6) **Tuning intensity — tested (E010→E011), bounded.** We probed whether the per-individual null is an artifact of light tuning: heavy LoRA (r=64/6ep, 4× capacity) leaves the null intact (per-subject +0.0004, CI [−0.0005,+0.0013], n=9; 79% carried by one subject, LOO-subject → +0.0001 = the light-regime value). But this is robustness to LoRA *capacity*, not to a stronger manipulation: heavy LoRA did **not** move the representation more (mse absolute uR² +0.0008 both regimes; ppl ~1.33× base both) — at fixed λ the alignment gradient is exhausted. The knob that *does* move it, λ_brain, wrecks perplexity (`E009`/`L017`: λ=30 → ppl 92) and still does not grow the gap. So **within the matched-perplexity regime the per-individual null cannot be escaped** by tuning intensity. We further tested the **objective axis**: an InfoNCE/NT-Xent **contrastive** brain-loss (Negi's loss family) run per-individual (n=9, same crossed inference) also gives a well-powered null at matched perplexity (mean −0.0002, CI [−0.0008,+0.0005]; `E013b`) — so the null is not an artifact of the MSE objective either (caveat: the 5-ROI target is low-dimensional for contrastive). With tuning-intensity and the objective both ruled out at ROI granularity, and the voxelwise *distillation-readout* lever shown not to take hold (§6(5)), the **one genuinely untested door is a different induction method** — full fine-tuning (not a LoRA distillation readout) on high-dimensional naturalistic voxelwise targets across a multi-subject cohort. The distinction matters for the power question: the distillation lever's failure is *n-independent* (a single-subject mechanism failure, §6(5)), so more subjects cannot rescue *it*; the ~5-deep-subject requirement (`L024`) applies only to the *full-FT* route, and only conditional on full-FT first inducing an above-base improvement at all. We have 3 deep subjects; that route is open but untested.

## 7. Conclusion

We offer a validation protocol — matched-perplexity baseline, permuted-fMRI twin, per-individual inference — that prior brain-tuning work does not run end-to-end, and we show it bites: under it, the alignment and downstream gains we sought are well-powered nulls. Brain-alignment is real and measurable in aggregate (powered A2), but optimizing it produces no per-individual brain-specific gain in our matched-perplexity distillation regime, and the apparent in-domain gain is an artifact of evaluating against a cross-subject-averaged target — the practice of the Tuckute benchmark we (and others) optimize against, and of group-average RSA. For practitioners, the rule is concrete: a brain-tuning result is not evidence of a brain-specific effect unless it is matched on perplexity, tested against a permuted-fMRI twin, and shown at the level of the individual brain — not a group average. The open frontier is whether a stronger objective+data regime (full fine-tuning + contrastive loss + multi-subject naturalistic fMRI) escapes this under the same protocol — a test for which the powered data does not yet exist.
